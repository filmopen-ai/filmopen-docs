# Jobs, bringing media in, the sync and renders

Work that outlives the page that asked for it, the one way media enters a film, and comparing its local files with a sharing target.

### 6.12 Application-owned jobs — `filmopen_jobs`

The engine and its contracts: `filmopen_jobs` is the pure-Dart package for application-owned jobs, independent of the film and its screens, with no runtime dependency and no app import. Its public barrel exports `job_api.dart` (hiding `copyJobData`), the `JobEngine` factory and interface in `job_engine.dart`, and `producer.dart`, plus only `refuseCredentialKeys` from `job_record.dart`. The API includes `JobView`, actions, requests, checkpoints, runner results and `JobRun`; the scheduler, records, journal, 30-second lease, storage and execution claims remain internal and are never exported. The app reaches execution through insertion and the state adapter; its local attention composition uses the already declared jobs edge without changing the shared package graph. State constructs and owns the application service; forms import only the public job views and actions they display.

Insertion owns a scoped view projection over each exact loaded record's id, kind and opaque payload. The generic engine supplies those existing values without interpreting producer recovery or render policy. The projection changes only state arguments; every other engine view field retains its ordinary meaning. Its private Zone door, `Symbol('filmopen.insertion.viewProjection')`, has type `Map<String, String> Function(String, String, Map<String, Object?>, Map<String, String>)`: the exact record id, kind, opaque payload and a copy of the state arguments. A present value of the wrong type leaves the ordinary unprojected view in place and reports `jobs.projectionIgnored` once per engine, without carrying the rejected value.

`JobView` equality covers all eleven presentation fields. State-argument maps compare by entries and action sets by membership, independently of insertion order; their hashes follow the same rule. The state adapter filters each snapshot by place and applies `distinct(listEquals)`, preserving job order while suppressing repeated equal snapshots, including repetitions of the initial seed and empty results. A changed field or order still publishes.

The engine factory is inert: construction performs no I/O, and explicit `submit` or `restore` activates work. Restoring one project leaves other projects dormant; the caller reconstructs project-specific bindings, which stay in memory. The engine retains `BindJob` and may invoke it after `restore` returns, when a later journal reload or lease takeover discovers work; each invocation must recheck its binding context. Explicit restore keeps journal observation active even when its initial load is empty, allowing later work to be discovered; session-only submission alone still performs no disk access. An explicit restore that meets an active attempt preserves that attempt's binding until its claim is released, then discards the old cached binding before another attempt starts. The next attempt reconstructs its binding through the latest retained binder. A binder that throws at attempt admission fails only that record: it waits as `bindingFailed`, with a safe error code and a one-second saved retry deadline. Its claim is released, no runner effect occurs, and eligible siblings continue in the same tick. Once Stop is accepted, a failed cleanup binding remains `stopping` with no second Stop action; it keeps its saved one-second retry deadline and pending cancellation intent until cleanup can reconstruct its binding. It is reported as `attemptFailed` with the exception's type and its whole message, as every report of the engine is. The caller selects session or durable persistence: session jobs touch no disk, even when a directory is configured, while each durable job has one JSON file. Clocks and storage are injected for tests. Credentials never belong in a payload; an insertion's payload keeps its names, file paths and URLs as they are (§6.13), and presentation names and preview paths are not persisted. A result can name the file it is about: `JobResult.done` and `JobResult.needsAttention` take a `displayName`, null by default, which the engine puts in the job's view when the attempt ends.

`refuseCredentialKeys(Map)` recursively checks payload maps and lists once, at each door a payload enters a record by: a submission, a checkpoint that carries a payload, and a record read from the file journal (kept out and reported as `unreadableRecord`); the engine reports a refused submission or checkpoint as `unsafePayload` and the caller receives the refusal. A record's own construction, change or copy does not walk the payload again, so a checkpoint without a payload or a phase change costs no walk. A refusal names the key it refused (`JobUnavailable(unsafePayload: key password)`), never its value. Insertion's and the sync's own checks of the payloads they make stay where they are. Key names are split into words across case, separators and digits; neighbouring word runs matching known credential names are refused. A `key`, `keys`, `token` or `tokens` tail, including trailing qualifiers such as `value` or `header`, is refused when its value contains nonempty text directly or inside a map or list. Refusal is `JobUnavailable('unsafePayload')`. Thus textual `apiKey`, `openai_token`, `Token`, `accessTokens` and payload `projectKey` are refused, while `tokenCount: 3`, `submission` and `project` are allowed. The rule does not inspect the record's own `projectKey` or `placeKey`, nor the lease's fencing token. The helper is the only exported declaration from the otherwise private record file.

The scheduler starts eligible jobs in `seq` order within each lane's capacity, with job and lane holds carrying their reason and actions. An exact capacity entry overrides prefix entries; otherwise the longest key ending in `*` whose preceding text matches the lane supplies its capacity. A lane without a matching entry has capacity one. A successful queued submission commit accepts the job and retains its runtime binding even if the following scheduling tick fails; `submit` returns that same job id and reports the scheduling failure, so a later tick can continue the accepted job. A diagnostic observer that throws cannot change that acceptance. Job and lane holds compose independently, and a due retry exposes its effective lane hold. Retry deadlines survive restart. A new lease generation reloads persisted records before scheduling or assigning a sequence, including when this engine owned an earlier lease. A stop or a lane hold is signalled to the active runner only after its intent is successfully persisted, durably in the journal for a durable job; a refused commit does not interrupt work. Acknowledgement never waits for the runner, external cancellation or lane capacity. Stored stop and hold intentions are honored before a recovered runner starts. There is no pause action: a person stops a job, and a hold comes from its request or its lane's policy. Registration declares `interruptible`, false by default. Holding a lane interrupts only active attempts of a kind registered as interruptible. If the kind has a cancellation callback, successful cleanup queues the job again with its checkpoints intact, no job hold of its own, and the lane policy's reason and actions. Without a cancellation callback, only a runner result of `cancelled` requeues the job under the hold; `done`, `failed`, `needsAttention` and `retry` retain their result. The consumed hold intent is cleared, preserving the retry deadline and the effective lane hold's actions. Releasing the lane permits a new attempt. An immediately released hold still interrupts the current attempt, and another lane is unaffected. A user stop wins over the hold, including during cleanup. Failed cleanup remains actionable and retry preserves the interruption's intent, so a hold retry cleans up then requeues instead of cancelling the job. A stale attempt's lease token fences its writes, and a nonblocking execution claim per job prevents a new attempt from overlapping an old effect that is still running, without occupying another job's lane slot. A job whose runner is unknown stays saved and needs attention, with retry/dismiss so a later build can execute it. Completed jobs expire after a day or are removed by dismissal. The expiry is the engine's own decision, and it reports it through the same callback as its failures: `expire` as it decides, with the job's kind and phase, when it finished (`doneAt`), its age, the rule (`doneForADay`) and the journal it leaves (`durable`, the job's file, or `session`); `expired` as the removal ends, with the same values, the outcome (`removed`, or `failed` with the error's type and text) and the milliseconds; a refused removal still fails the tick, and the record stays until a later tick removes it.

`register(kind, runner, cancel:..., interruptible: false)` accepts an optional cancellation callback and allows a kind to opt into lane-hold interruption. The running attempt retains the policy captured when it starts. A stopped or lane-held job that previously started runs that cleanup under its execution claim before becoming cancelled or requeued respectively, including when a persisted interruption is recovered. After successful intent persistence, a queued job that never started cancels without invoking either callback. Cleanup effects still persist intent and obey the lease, but do not wait for lane capacity or lane holds; a busy execution claim leaves cleanup pending. A cleanup refusal or exception leaves the job needing attention. Retry preserves its stop or hold intent and runs cleanup alone before deciding whether to cancel or requeue, never the ordinary runner during that cleanup. A cleanup that answers a held `waiting` — a waiting result without a retry delay — has released its stop: the producer still holds the work, and the job waits with that result's code and actions, its stop intent cleared and no cleanup pending, so its Retry runs the ordinary runner, and a Stop after that runs the cleanup again. A delayed retry from a cleanup is not held: it needs attention with its intent, as a refusal does. A cleanup's result, once committed, is reported as `cleanupResult` — except a lane hold's requeue, which keeps its own path: the kind, the phase and state code before and after, what the cleanup answered (its phase, code and retry delay), the intent, the intent and pending flag left, the actions, the decision (`held`, `cancelled` or `attention`) and its reason.

Uploads register as interruptible: an interrupted import takes back what it copied, and the job imports anew once the hold is released. Producers do not: a producer is never interrupted by a hold, so an active producer under a hold continues working and attaches its output normally. `ProducerRun.cancel()` answers whether the producer is over: `true` where it is over, `false` where the same work is still held and can be followed again, with fresh subscriptions and a repeatable cancellation; a refusal is a throw. The render's run reads the answer from the manager's state after its stop, so a stop left unconfirmed, or refused while the render finishes, answers `false`. The held lane blocks subsequent jobs, including producers, until release. A policy hold never restarts paid work.

`Producer` and `ProducerRun` are contracts used by deterministic stand-ins and the application’s managed render producer. Producers open their own byte streams through `ProducedItem.open`; consumers receive no fetch URL. The consumer starts or restores a run, observes its state, invokes cancellation only through committed cleanup, and acknowledges after keeping its output.

The lease is renewed only after one third of its lifetime has passed since its last stamp (10 seconds by default); acquisition and a guarded effect still stamp immediately. Per-job claim files are swept after journal loading and terminal completion, retaining nonterminal jobs and any claim currently held. Claim acquisition and sweep share the folder's permanent `claims.lock`, with a local queue for separate instances and an OS lock for separate processes. No process can open a claim between the sweep's close and deletion. A locally held inode is not reopened even to probe it, because closing another descriptor can drop a POSIX lock. Cleanup failures report `lockNotRemoved` without preventing scheduling; the permanent coordination inode remains. An attempt retains whether it is durable until its claim is released, so dismissing its terminal record while release drains still triggers the final lock sweep.

The journal's internal `JournalWrites` seam (`journal_io.dart`) makes write failures testable; its production implementation, `DiskJournalWrites`, flushes a temporary file in the destination's directory before renaming it into place. Six fault tests cover a partial temporary write or flush failure, a rename failure and a failure after rename, each for an initial write and a replacement. A replacement leaves the old or new complete record, never a partial record; an initial write leaves no record before rename, or the complete new record after it. Unsupported schemas and malformed records are preserved on disk and reported through the factory's optional `onJournalProblem(id, code, [error])` callback, rather than loaded as jobs, at every load that keeps it out: one restore loads twice, and a window without the lease loads every second, each load reporting it again. The callback also reaches the engine for session and durable jobs: unexpected runner failures report `attemptFailed`, scheduled tick failures report `tickFailed`, and refused action persistence reports `actionFailed`. Its full signature is `onJournalProblem(id, code, [error, data])`: a failure carries `error` and no `data`; an event — the expiry of a done job, `expire` and `expired` — carries `data`, the values the engine worked on, and `error` beside them where the event failed. Every failure is reported each time it happens, so a repeated failure stays in the log; a sink that wants fewer lines filters them itself. The serial helper reports nothing; its callers report their own failure codes. The callback's optional error argument is the error's runtime type and its whole message (`StateError: Bad state: …`, the type said once where the text already starts with it), never its stack; a runner must not put a key in an exception's text. The state adapter (`logJobReport`) writes `jobs.<code>` with the job id: a failure as a warning with that error, an event at info with its values, an event that failed as a warning with both.

### 6.13 Media insertion — `filmopen_insertion`

Insertion is separate from the storage and playback package (§6.9). `filmopen_insertion` is pure Dart; its permitted application dependencies are `filmopen_jobs`, `filmopen_media`, `filmopen_store` and `filmopen_format`. It imports their public barrels, never state, forms or the plug-in host. The moved importer, thumbnailer, stock thumbnails and local-file/off-thread IO/stub pairs keep their existing responsibilities. The public barrel exports the service, target, source and environment contracts and the legacy importer/thumbnail APIs; the runtime, replay helpers and journal checks remain private. The transitional `importMedia` facade and export were removed after architectural review when forms switched to the service.

**The target is independent of the visible page.** `InsertionTarget` captures the film key, entity stem, accepted media kinds, purpose and a `TargetPlace`: `SlotPlace` names a group of the entity's `media` (`MediaSlot.group`) with its first/last insertion policy, `BlockPlace` names one of a block's six media groups (§10.3) by the block's `id` and the group, with the `spans` chosen before the render or upload — by the place's chooser, once before any road (§9.3) — kept out of its identity, since one block group is one place whatever its entries cover — and its target's accepted kinds default to that group's (`acceptedKindsFor`), and `TakesOnly` names no entity field; `TargetPlace.group` is the group a place's outputs become entries of — the slot's, the block's — and null for `TakesOnly`. **No kind chooses the destination**: every output of a job becomes an entry of the place's group, as one block in selection order, and a place of another kind of slot is refused (`invalidOutputType`). These values round-trip as JSON. The target serializes its film key as `project`; the Dart property remains `projectKey`. The `placeKey` combines film, stem and canonical destination identity; insertion order is retained as policy but does not split one destination into different places.

**A durable description does not preserve source access.** `FilesSource` retains runtime inputs and copies byte lists. Its `SourceDescription` records each input as it is: its order, its kind (file, bytes or URL), its extension, its original name, and a file's path or a URL's address; bytes stay in memory. `ProducerSource` snapshots its request in memory and describes only its producer kind. Generated take file names, measured sizes and hashes are persisted as ingest intent. Every checkpoint and effect intent is logged as `insertion.journal.write` with the job's id, its stage and phase and the whole payload, so that a job can be followed from the log alone; the jobs engine's `refuseCredentialKeys` is the one check of a payload. **A producer's outputs made by a plug-in's run** — a render's, every one of whose items names the same `run` (`{ tag, stem, hook: "render" }`, the stem a plug-in's that carries the tag) and the same record of what it was asked (`item`) — are the run's: their takes are named under the viewer's handle for the plug-in (`john123.zi`), their record is a `kind: "plugin"` batch by that handle with the run's `plugin` and `hook`, each item carrying what the run was asked — §12.2's keys of it alone, so that nothing a producer says overrides what the file measures or the place — and neither the plug-in's file name nor a nested `provenance`; the payload keeps the run's three names once planned, never what it was asked, and a plan that names no run is an import whatever its outputs say after a restart. Outputs that name no run are an import as before; outputs that name a run otherwise — a tag that is no tag, a hook that is not `render`, a stem that is not the tag's plug-in's, no record, or runs and records that differ between them — are refused (`invalidPayload`) before anything is written: whose they are is never guessed. The payload names its idempotency identifier `submission`. The temporary journals with the former `token` and target `projectKey` payload keys have no compatibility reader. A producer's recovery JSON is journaled as the producer gives it. `JobUnavailable` codes thrown by output capabilities, a producer's state codes and a store's codes reach the view and the journal as they are.

**One binding is captured before submission yields.** `InsertionBinding` holds the project, viewer, guards, locale, media store and size limit — the web's 64 MiB (`maxImportBytesWeb`, the app's own policy, since a web session holds every upload in memory), none on the desktop; the limit is part of its fingerprint, so a job persisted under the old desktop limit of 2 GiB is refused `bindingChanged` once. Its identity fingerprint covers the project key, viewer, `guards.warnOnly` and byte limit, and the payload stores it. Store availability and the manifest's old `data` field are not part of the fingerprint: media belongs to the bound project's own source. A new film or person on screen cannot redirect a running copy. Restoring unfinished mutation requires the same film and identity fingerprint; a changed viewer, guard policy or byte limit remains `bindingChanged`. A validated started producer whose complete attachment has already been verified and marked may restore acknowledgement alone in the same film, as described below. An inactive job may rebind to the current locale and machine settings. Active attempts retain the capture with which they started. The binding also carries the app's write record (`InsertionBinding.record`, §7.5): every take record, batch and cleanup the job writes goes through `InsertionBinding.writer()`, which enters each write in the record before the source is asked, so that the next publication lays it over the index unread. The binding carries, where this platform has an engine, `probeAudio`: whether the clip at a local path has sound (an `AudioProbe`: `audio`, or the engine's `code` and `message`), the probe of the engine the timeline measures with (`mediaAudioProbeProvider`, §19.13), read only when a clip is imported. Both roads measure a clip once, after its bytes are written, through the store's local path: the direct road in `_importOne` and the replay in `_one` for a take it wrote; the answer goes on the item as `audio` — the record's and the checkpoint's — and `entryOf` writes it on the entry (Project Specification §9.0); no engine (`no_probe`, the probe's `unavailable` included), media held without a local path (`no_file`, told by the source's disk path without reading the clip) or an engine that fails to read the file (`failed:<code>`, logged at warning as `media.audio_probe.failed` with the file, the code and the engine's message) leaves it absent, and the clip is imported unmeasured: the probe's failure is not the import's. `media.imported` carries `audio`, `audio_reason` and `audio_ms` for a clip. `InsertionEnvironment` supplies capture, `noticeWritten` (one publication with a listing for the film, where it is still open), the clock and the entity patch seam without an import of application state.

**Restoration binds the opening before it waits.** The initial `restore` call captures its opening snapshot synchronously, uses it while journal loading runs, then clears the temporary closure capture. The retained engine binder may be called on later reloads or takeovers and then captures the live film. A closed or different film returns `projectNotOpen`, leaving the attachment waiting for its film; a changed identity fingerprint remains `bindingChanged` unless the validated job needs only acknowledgement of its already verified attachment in that same film. Existing inactive runtime bindings also receive the newly captured environment while retaining available source capabilities. An opaque or malformed place key, or another job kind, cannot make insertion throw or resume unrelated work.

**Exact accepted identity.** `InsertionService.insert` has an optional, defaulted `onAccepted` notification. It reports one exact actionable insertion id once, after the engine accepts the submission. A throwing observer cannot undo acceptance, and is logged `insertion.acceptedObserverFailed` with its message. Stop uses the existing `act(id, stop)` door. `insert` and `submit(target, source)` are one door logged around — `submit` without the notification — answering the job's id and throwing a refusal; logged `insertion.submit.start {target, kind}` (a producer's `request` beside, an upload's `files` — each input's kind, name, extension and path or address), then `insertion.submit {id, ms}`, `insertion.submit.refused {code, target, name, ms}` (`name` the refused file's, where there is one) or `insertion.submit.failed {target, error, message, ms}`.

**Recovery association.** At every producer checkpoint and effect intent, insertion projects the source's producer kind and the recovery's `id` into the reserved `producerKind` and `renderId` state arguments. Restoration recomputes this projection from the exact loaded insertion record, including terminal records and binding refusals. An id-only recovery is sufficient. Persisted association arguments are never authority: a record with no producer kind or no string `id` has them removed and leaves the job generic. Unrelated state arguments and every other engine view field are preserved. No place, entity or row order supplies an association.

**The service owns the job, and callers can observe its place.** `InsertionService` supplies `insert`, `jobsFor`, `views`, `act`, `restore`, `filmClosed` and `close`, backed by the jobs engine's upload/producer runners. The application configures eight active attempts per film's `insertion:<film>` admission lane. The journal retains that lane name across restoration. An attempt observes its producer under its live execution claim while queued or running at the provider; its subscription, checkpoints and shutdown draining remain the attempt's. The provider's `queued` state is presented as `queued` with `waitingToRender`, `running` as `working`, and collection as `working` with `collecting`. Local execution limits belong to the producer's resource scheduler. A producer's mutations share a separate FIFO gate keyed by film: allocation before reservations are copied, filesystem checks and reservation, ingest, batch publication, attachment, failed-producer rollback and stopped-producer settlement; an upload does not join it (below). Provider start, restore, observation, stopped-output collection and acknowledgement do not hold this gate. Gate ownership spans services draining in the same isolate; a queued mutation checkpoints `waitingForFilm` while another job of the same film holds the section, keeping Stop available during normal work. Its insertion tile and Activity row say that it is waiting for another job in this film, in the chosen language. The FIFO waiter checkpoints that waiting state once when it joins the queue, then awaits the preceding mutation without a heartbeat or timeout of its own. Cleanup waits safely with no further actions. It checkpoints its active claim again after acquisition before any file effect. A refused waiting checkpoint retains its FIFO link until the earlier writer leaves; only a process restart frees a disk write that never returns. Closing or restoring a service in the same isolate does not clear that film's shared FIFO tail. Producer ingest and attachment recheck the captured film’s ownership after acquiring the gate. A closed session waits for its film; a replaced session retains its producer result for Stop without writing a new take or batch. Media attachment rechecks that ownership too, with its actions governed by whether the producer has already been acknowledged. Existing per-entity patch and draft settlement remain the only document writer. `InsertionService.current` starts as `NoInsertionService`; `main` installs the state-adapted application service (§7). Durable film keys select durable jobs; session keys select session jobs, which never enter the journal even if the engine has a directory. `insert` asked while the plug-ins' settings are read again — a plug-in allowed a moment ago — is refused at once as `settingsLoading` (the environment's `capture` throws it), logged `insertion.bind.refused` with the code, the reason, the target and the source's description; nothing is submitted, its place says the plug-ins are not ready, and the person asks again (§7). Unsupported upload kinds are refused before a job or a write, by `acceptsFile`, the rule a place judges its files by before it submits them (§5.10, §9.3). **A person's files take the direct road** (`MediaImporter.import`, below): each file is read, hashed and stored once as it passes, the copy's fraction checkpointed for its tile (below), its thumbnail and the batch record written, then one checkpoint `attaching` keeps each take's file name, its item number and its measured `width` and `height` with the record's stem, and the attachment follows; *Attach again* and a restart attach from that checkpoint alone, never importing again. A failure during the import takes back what it copied (`media.importFailed`); the job needs attention with the refusal's code and the file's name, *Retry* importing anew, or ends `failed` with `recordNotWritten`. A stop during the import is seen between the chunks of the file being copied, so that file is never renamed into the media folder and its temporary goes, and the files the batch had finished are taken back (`media.importCancelled`); the job ends `cancelled`. Its files live in this session's memory: a restart before the checkpoint needs attention as `sourceUnavailable`, and what the interrupted import had copied stays in the media folder as it is — a take with its record where the import had finished, never attached. A stop after the record keeps the batch and its takes, unattached — the stop refuses the attachment's patch — and publishes them through the controller's `noticeWritten`, the batch laid over the index from the record its writer entered it in, so the person finds them under *Takes*. The upload logs `insertion.upload.start {id, target, source, viewer, reserved}`, `insertion.upload.imported {id, batch, items, ms}`, `insertion.upload.refused {id, code, name, message, ms}` (or `{id, code: sourceUnavailable, reason, ms}`), `insertion.upload.stopped {id, reason, ms}` (with `batch` and `items` after the record), `insertion.upload.attachAgain {id, batch, reason}`, `insertion.upload.failed {id, stage, code, error, message, ms}` for an unexpected failure — answered `writeFailed` — or a refusal thrown, and `insertion.upload.done {id, phase, code, ms}`. For a producer's outputs the internal runtime checkpoints intent before effects; the replay importer reserves the batch and take names before reading, then records each expected size and SHA-256 before writing. Replay reuses only a saved name whose bytes match both measurements, and checks the batch's identity and item facts before accepting an existing record. A producer's output lost across restart needs attention as `sourceUnavailable`; completed takes can be reconciled without its private source access. **The record comes before the settlement** (Project Specification §12.3): once every output is written, the batch record is written — each item numbered by its output's place in the order given (a render's by its place in the plug-in's list; an output whose take was not written leaving a gap), and each naming the place's `group` (none for `TakesOnly`) — and only then is the ingest settled and the attachment begun. While the record is being written the set of outputs is fixed (`recordPending`), so a restart never tries a failed item again; a restart that finds the record writes nothing and checks its items (`_sameItems`), and one that cannot write it — a store's refusal, `nameTaken`, or a runtime input lost across a restart, whose original name and provenance the record takes (`sourceUnavailable`) — rolls back every output of the job, a producer's partial ingest included, and ends the job `failed` with `recordNotWritten` and *Dismiss* alone. A settled job's record is checked again on every replay, a changed one refused as `nameTaken` without rolling anything back. **A stop asked before a producer's record stops the ingest**: its outputs, which a render paid for, are recorded as far as they were written, `cancelled`, or taken back where no record can be written; a stop asked while the record is being written stops only the attachment, since that write is fenced by its checkpoint and not refused by a stop. **A stop no attempt settled** — one recovered after a restart, or asked of a job no attempt is running — **settles a producer's outputs the same way** before the producer is cancelled: its run, restored or heard again, is asked for its outputs once more — a render's are still staged — and every take on disk as the plan measured it, its write noted before or not, is recorded as far as it was written, else taken back where the producer hands nothing over or the record cannot be written, so that no take is left without its record. A producer that says nothing within `producerHeardWithin` (30 seconds) leaves the stop to be tried again (`producerOutcomeUnknown`) with nothing taken back, as does a folder out of reach (`rootUnavailable`), the producer kept to hand its outputs over then; one whose work is not restored at all leaves its outcome unknown, the job waiting for the person with nothing left on disk; where the version is gone, nothing is recorded and the producer is stopped as before, what it wrote left where it is. A stop the running attempt settled stops the producer without settling again, and one tried again after its own settlement still stops it (`stopSettled`); a stop asked after the ingest had settled acknowledges the producer, whose outputs were kept, as the run would have (`producerAcknowledged`, kept in the journal) — after a restart too, from its confirmed marker or by acknowledging the same restored run; unconfirmed acknowledgement is recorded as uncertain, the captured film is told what was written and the job still ends cancelled, without claiming failed filesystem cleanup — unless its film had no folder and another film has since replaced it, when the stop lets the producer go; a producer already told is never stopped, since it holds nothing more. **A stop the producer did not take** — its cancellation answers that the same work is still held, a render whose stop is unconfirmed or was refused while it finished — leaves the job waiting as `stopUnconfirmed` with *Retry* and *Dismiss*, never `cancelled`: *Retry* runs the job's ordinary attempt again, which follows the same run — reused in the session, restored from its saved recovery after a restart — and places what it hands over once, as any attempt does; *Dismiss* removes the job. A Stop after that *Retry* asks the producer again. Where a version is gone or no viewer is bound, the stopped settlement records nothing and logs `insertion.settleSkipped` with `versionUnavailable` or `viewerUnavailable` for what is missing. The cleanup logs `insertion.cleanup.start {id, stage, producerKind, producerRecovery, producerCancelled, kept}` before it waits on anything, and `insertion.cleanup {id, stage, producerKind, producerRecovery, cancelAsked, over, kept, decision, code, ms}` at every exit, the decision `cancelled`, `stillHeld`, `attention`, `failed`, or `interrupted` where a lost lease hands the cleanup to the next attempt; an attempt logs `insertion.produce.attached {id, how, stage, producerKind, producerRecovery, producerCancelled}`, `how` saying whether it `reused` the session's run, `restored` it from its recovery or `started` it. **A replay whose producer can no longer hand its outputs over** — a render that failed or was stopped elsewhere, or that its manager no longer holds (`producerFailed`) — takes back what the job wrote before the restart, as a stop does, a record written before it standing as it is, and ends with the producer's code. **A film with no folder that another film has replaced** — the bundled sample's session copy, left for another film — is never written with a producer's outputs: the job, once they are there, needs attention as `filmClosed`, the outputs kept with it until the person stops it, which lets them go — a producer told before a closing refused its attachment holds none, and its job offers *Dismiss* — and a producer's attachment refused as not open ends the same way rather than waiting for a film that never opens again; a producer's outputs are held back too while no film is open — closed, or another loading — the job waiting for its film (`projectNotOpen`, with *Attach again* and **Stop**, or *Dismiss* once its producer was told) rather than writing into a copy it cannot tell is kept, and the film another one replaces asks their *Attach again* once to say so; a render is never told its outputs were kept while its film is gone, and the application says, as each such job ends, that it did (§9.7); an upload, which nothing paid for, waits as it always has, one closed and not replaced — *Close project* keeps it — is waited for as before, and a folder film is written to whether it is open or not. **A take written before a retry or a restart whose bytes no longer match what was measured is no longer the job's**: neither recorded nor taken back, while a producer's other outputs are recorded as far as they were written (`partialIngest`); any other failure to read it leaves the job to be tried again, the take still the job's. **A record on disk is never undone**: one written before a restart the journal did not hear of, or before a failure the stop then met, stands as it is, and nothing it names is taken back — on a stop, and on a replay that finds a take it names changed, which ends `nameTaken` as a settled job's does. A rollback frees the set of outputs again, so a later attempt ingests anew. A journal whose target no longer decodes — the retired legacy place, the old slot discriminators — ends `invalidPayload`, dismissable; it is not migrated. Each take written logs `media.imported` once — an upload's with its stem, kind, original name, where it came from, byte count, SHA-256 and whether its thumbnail was made, a render's replay with its stem, kind and byte count; attachment retry does not emit another. A producer's ingest can keep successful items and report `partialIngest`.

**Code that inserts reads one job.** `jobView(id)` answers one job's view, read-only, and `needsAttention` is a phase like any other; the default service answers none, and the application's service projects the view with its render attention as it projects its views. It logs `insertion.lookup.start` and `insertion.lookup.done {found, ms}` — never the id asked, a caller's string until it is found. A done job its place has drawn is dismissed by that place (§9.3) and leaves the views, and then has no view.

**Reopening attaches nothing on its own.** A job waiting for its film (`projectNotOpen`, with *Attach again*) waits for the person's *Attach again* after the film opens again; `restore` logs `insertion.restore.start {film, durable}` and `insertion.restored {film, jobs, replaced, ms}` with each job's phase and stage. The one exception is a render waiting for a film with no folder that the opening replaced, whose place never shows again: the opening asks its *Attach again* once (`insertion.replacedFilm {id, film, accepted, code, reason}`), which ends it `filmClosed` with its Stop, so that the application says so (§9.7).
**Runtime inputs end with their job.** Successful terminal persistence releases the engine's binding, and terminal restore does not reconstruct one. The insertion service releases its private source and producer cache when a job becomes terminal or is dismissed. A failed terminal commit retains the binding for retry; unresolved jobs retain available runtime capabilities. Closing releases remaining in-memory ownership.

**Attachment is proved by the patch's hand-back** (`insertion_attach.dart`). The service patches through `InsertionEnvironment.patchEntity`, and `InsertionPatchResult` succeeds only with the entity JSON as the writer wrote it, accepted by the attachment's own check; nothing is read for it. Each output becomes a `MediaEntry` of the place's group through `withEntriesInserted` — `reference` its take's stem, `batch` the record's stem and `item` its `n` there, and in `voice_samples` the `voice_binding` its run's output gave it (Project Specification §9.1: the platform, the preview it is or the voice that spoke it), read as the format reads one, planned with the job so that a restart attaches the same, and nothing else an output says of its entry — as one block, first or last as the place says; a reference the document already holds anywhere — in any group of the entity or of any block — is not added again. **The entry carries its facts** (§9.0), read from the job's payload item, where the replay planned them so that an attach after a restart writes the same and nothing is invented: `duration_ms` 0 for a still, else the plug-in's reported value, else absent; `width` and `height` as the replay measured a picture, else as reported, else absent — the item keeps of the plug-in's report its three whole numbers alone; `stage` the stage the render was asked at, absent on an upload; `unreviewed: true` on every render's output, a voice preview included, and never on an upload; `kept_at` never. A `BlockPlace`'s outputs go first into its group of the first block of that `id` (no such block, or a `media` that is not an object, is `wrongShape`), each with the `spans` chosen where the group allows that shape and else the group's default — a disallowed shape is dropped, never refused — and no `preview`; the patch's key is `blocks`. A render answering two kinds is attached whole into its one place: a clip and a soundtrack for one block are two insertions, each into its group. Media reaches `done` only when that document names every intended reference. A stale or refused attachment offers `attachAgain`, which retries attachment without another put or render — a producer's after verifying its existing take and batch, an upload's from its checkpoint. `projectNotOpen` leaves the job waiting for the person's *Attach again*. Successful items from a partial producer result are attached, while the job retains `partialIngest` and the missing items' outcome. `TakesOnly` tells the captured film after ingest (`InsertionEnvironment.noticeWritten`: one publication with a listing, the batch laid over the index unread, nothing read) and finishes without patching an entity; an attachment to a form publishes the batch with the patch's own gesture.

**The app owns insertion independently of its cards.** Forms submit a destination and source through the service, then draw public `JobView` data and invoke only the supplied actions. The former `importMedia` facade is gone. The plug-in host submits its render outputs through `InsertionService` with a `ProducerSource`; the application supplies `ManagedRenderProducer`, and each output’s group is retained through the insertion contract (§6.11).

**The direct import path** is an upload's road. It retains its collision refusal and does not join the service's mutation gate; it shares the service's reservation set (`reserved:`), adding the batch id it proposes before anything is awaited, so that two imports in flight together never take one id while neither record is in the index (`media.batchIdTaken` where a take of an id is in the store already, `media.importStart` with the id, the inputs, the groups and the reservations, `media.importDone` with the items). It is `MediaImporter.import(entity, inputs, viewer:, groups:, run:, cancelled:, reserved:, onProgress:)`, `groups` one per input — the group its item records, or null — the takes and the batch the viewer's for an upload, and `<viewer>.<tag>`'s, with a `kind: "plugin"` batch, for a plug-in's render, `run` being its `PluginRun`. A `MediaInput` is a file on this machine, bytes the caller has, or a URL (`http`/`https`, the content type checked before anything is stored, fetched with a client of its own — `debugMediaHttpClient`'s stand-in in a test — logged `media.fetchStart {url, name}` and `media.fetchAnswered {url, from, status, contentType, length, ms}`, `from` where redirects the client followed ended elsewhere; the client closed once whatever the outcome). Every kind is settled before a byte is written; then, per file, in this order: the bytes stream into the store while their SHA-256 and their size are measured — a picture small enough to thumbnail is kept as it passes, a clip is not, so a file larger than memory copies through — then the thumbnail into the project folder, then, once every file is in, the batch record through `ProjectWriter.writeImportBatch`, its items numbered `1..k` in the order given, the take's file number staying per purpose. A picture too big to hold has no `width` and `height` in its item, which §12.2 makes the right answer: measured on ingest, never invented. The importer **never touches an entity's file**: it answers the `ProjectMedia`. The insertion service attaches through the state's bound patch door, which uses the open draft when present, preserving autosave, the identity lock and stale refusals. **A record that cannot be written refuses the import**: the files and thumbnails the action wrote are removed and `recordNotWritten` is thrown, so no take arrives without its record (§12.3). Refusals are codes (`MediaImportProblem`: `notMedia`, `empty`, `tooLarge`, `nameTaken`, `rootUnavailable`, `noViewer`, `badUrl`, `unreadable` — a file gone between the picker and the import — and `recordNotWritten`), never sentences, and neither a URL nor a server's words ever reach an exception or a message; the log carries them (`media.importRefused` with the address, and for an answer that is not 200 its `status`, `reason`, `contentType`, redirect `location` and its body whole, for a 200 refused as not media, empty or declared too large its `status` and `contentType`, and for one refused as its bytes pass — too large or empty — the address; `media.fetchFailed` with the address and the error's message). **A stop is seen between chunks**: `cancelled` is read before each file, before each chunk is hashed and after its hash, and after each file's put; seen in the stream it ends the stream with `MediaImportCancelled`, a `WriteCancelled` of the store's, so the store's write closes its temporary, renames nothing and deletes it before it returns — logged `folder.write.cancelled {kind, path, step, bytes, waitMs, ms, reason}` at info, not `folder.writeFailed` — and the path is never added to what the batch wrote; the files the batch finished, and a file whose put ended as the stop came, are taken back, then `media.importCancelled {batch, stem, stage, bytes, of, inputs, written, thumbnails, rollback, notRemoved, ms, reason}` at info. A stop inside one chunk (4 MiB from a file on this machine) is not seen until the chunk has passed, and a write waiting in the media queue behind another polls nothing until it enters. An unexpected failure keeps `media.importFailed` at warning, with its stage, the file and its bytes. **A file on this machine is read in chunks of 4 MiB** (`localReadChunkBytes`), not 64 KiB: the app pays a wait for every chunk on its own isolate, so a large clip copies in few, large chunks. The read logs `media.localRead.open {path, chunkBytes, reason}` and `media.localRead.end {path, outcome: complete | cancelled | failed, chunks, bytes, ms, error, message}`, at warning when the file failed; `cancelled` means its reader stopped listening (a Stop, or a failure further on, which logs its own cause). **The hash is taken off the painting thread**: `MediaImporter.startHasher` starts one `ChunkHasher` per file — a worker isolate on every platform but the web, where the stub hashes inline as the bytes pass — each chunk sent, its acknowledgement awaited, then handed to the store, so one chunk is in flight; the digest is taken once at the end and the hasher ended in every case, logged `media.hash.start {batch, take, input, backend, of}` and `media.hash.done {batch, take, input, backend, bytes, ms, outcome: complete | stopped | failed, error, message}`, its `ms` the file's whole copy — read, hash, acknowledgement and write. A render's replay goes through the same measure, with no stop and no progress of its own. **An upload's tile shows its copy's progress** (`onProgress:`): bytes written across the selection's files over the lengths they declare before a byte is read — `0` as the copy starts, then at most once a second when it has moved 1 % or more, then `1` when the last file's copy ends, before the thumbnails and the record — each one logged `media.importProgress {batch, stem, fraction, bytes, of, ms, reason: start | tick | end}` as it is given and checkpointed with that progress — its state read as the journal writes it: `working` with *Stop*, or, once a stop is asked, `stopping` with no action, so that a fraction queued behind a Stop's write never puts *Stop* back, logged `insertion.journal.write {id, stage, phase, payload, progress, reason: progress | stopping}` —, a checkpoint refused logged `insertion.checkpointFailed {id, stage: progress, fraction, error, message}` at warning; a selection with a file that declares no length — an address — gets none and keeps the spinner (`media.importStart` says which, `progress` and `progressReason`). **A film closed while its upload copies** — or another film opened in its place — is told to the service by the application's binding with the film it was (`filmClosed`), logged `insertion.binding.filmChanged {from, to, closed, project}`, and the runtime logs `insertion.filmClosed {film, durable, jobs: [{id, phase, stateCode}], decision: uploadsRunOn, reason}`, its jobs matched by the film their place names; nothing stops, and an upload that ends with its film closed waits for *Attach again*.

**The thumbnail** (`filmopen_insertion/thumbnailer.dart`, §6.3): within 300 × 300, no crop and no enlarging, EXIF orientation baked in, transparency flattened on the theme-neutral grey `#808080`, JPEG at quality 80 or 60 when it exceeds 48 KB. Decoding runs through `Isolate.run` on IO platforms; without a worker, the derivative defers to stock artwork. Checked PNG chunk streams with one IHDR, JPEG SOF, GIF canvas, supported INFO-family BMP and static WebP headers measure positive source dimensions without allocating the decoded bitmap. Each axis is at most `0x7fffffff`, and the source area is at most maxThumbnailPixels; the area check divides the pixel bound by one positive axis instead of multiplying untrusted dimensions. Dimensions outside these bounds yield neither measured dimensions nor a photograph thumbnail. BMP palettes and offsets are bounded; WebP chunks, competing headers and actual encoded dimensions are checked, with raw VP8 scaling bits refused. CORE/short BMP layouts and animated WebP without a proven frame/canvas bound defer to stock. Unknown or malformed bounds never fall through to an allocating decoder probe. The current decoder has no reduced JPEG or PNG mode, so supported pictures decode whole on the IO worker up to maxThumbnailPixels of 80 million source pixels, without an additional decoder-input byte ceiling; beyond that pixel bound they use stock artwork, while the importer still drops retained picture bytes above 128 MB. Dimensions that pass these checks remain measured ingest facts when a later derivative step defers. Bounded supported pictures decode their first frame alone, retaining existing orientation, colour, fitting and transparency. The PNG library has no decompressed-stream output ceiling, so these guards do not establish a complete malformed-inflate memory bound. The importer takes the decoded thumbnail where a held picture decodes within these bounds, else the stock artwork of its kind; a decoder that throws is logged (`media.thumbnailFailed`, code `decode`) and gets the stock artwork too, and each take logs, after the write, `media.thumbnail {stem, kind, picture, source: decoded | stock, reason, bytes, decodeMs, ms}`, the reason one of `decoded`, `not a picture`, `picture not held`, `no decoder on this platform`, `not decodable within the bounds` and `decoder threw`. Deferral supplies stock artwork now and creates no regeneration queue; only the importer stores the result. Clips, sounds and other files retain their existing stock artwork, stored in `thumbnails/` like any other.

Partial producer ingest settles its successful indexes and per-item failure codes durably before creating the batch, whose `status` is `done` where every output was ingested, `failed` — an ingest problem, with the items that were written — where some were not, and `cancelled` — a stop, with the items that were written — where a stop ended it. Recovery and attachment retry keep that subset and do not retry missing producer outputs; no acknowledgement releases their source. A missing batch whose original names/provenance have been lost holds as `sourceUnavailable`, preserving takes. Existing batches are checked against durable item facts without reconstructing private names. Malformed planned paths, identities, indexes or measured facts are refused before replay performs any effect.

Importers store takes directly in `media/` and record their file names as batch `output`. The insertion service uses each take's extensionless stem as its entity reference. Batch documents are written into `data/`; thumbnails use `thumbnails/tn_<stem>.jpg` relative to the project root. Replay uses the same file names and folder rules.

### 6.14 The sync — `filmopen_sync`

`filmopen_sync` is pure Dart, depending on `filmopen_format`, `filmopen_jobs` and `crypto`. Its public contract describes a `SyncTarget` through `inventory`, `find`, `committed`, streamed `put` and streamed `fetch` operations, with entries, receipts and stable failure codes. `SyncFailure` distinguishes a retry, a required sign-in and a permanent failure. The package supplies these contracts, lane and retry rules, inventories and differences, payload validation, a folder target, local byte streams and `SyncService` with its transfer runners. `SyncEnvironment` supplies the current source, target and clock through a memory-only `SyncBinding`, with `isOpen(projectKey)` identifying the matching open, nonloading film independently of its target. The state's `createApplicationSyncService` constructs an inert engine with `rules.capacities`, an application-supplied `targetFor` seam and a two-second settle by default. A supplied sync directory holds the journal, with the ledger in its `ledger` child and media parts in its `parts` child; without a directory these stores are in memory, and session films always use the service's separate memory stores. Storage construction touches no file; the entrypoint installs it after insertion, as described below. `ContainerSyncEnvironment` captures only an open, nonloading film with the matching key and an available target. `syncJobsProvider` subscribes before seeding and suppresses equal per-film snapshots. `SyncAppBinding` coalesces notifications in a microtask: opening restores, later changes reconcile, closing captures nothing and stops no jobs, and reopening restores (including its reconcile and waking `filmClosed` work). Loading makes the binding unavailable; readiness restores even the same film so sends that waited as `filmClosed` wake again. An ordinary write reread stays ready and reconciles. Its idempotent `close` cancels the arrival timer, detaches project listeners and starts arrival-subscription cancellation and service shutdown together, with a ten-second default grace period through `ExitTasks`. Subscription cleanup cannot delay the start of the service grace. Journal and service reports log `sync.<code>` with the job's id — the film's key where the report is the film's, as `ledgerUnreadable` is — the `error` the report carries, as it is: from the jobs engine and its journal, the error's type and its whole message; the engine's expiry of a done send, `expire` and `expired`, at info with its values, through the same `logJobReport` as the insertion's.

`LocalInventory.read` lists a `ProjectSource` once and measures each retained file: the root manifest, flat files in `data/` and flat files in `media/`. Thumbnails, nested files and other paths are excluded. Documents, including the manifest, are hashed with MD5 — from the text `SyncService(heldTexts:)` gives where it holds the document and that text's UTF-8 length is the file's, unread, and read once otherwise — a byte-order mark the reader dropped gives the held text another length, and so does a change made outside the app that changes the length; a change outside the app of the same length is hashed from the held text until the folder watch reads it again; media bytes are never read for an inventory. The application gives the open film's view (§7.5): the published index with the app's landed writes, so the local side is the film as every decision reads it; each pass logs `sync.inventory` with every path's hash or size and the documents it read. When a document's send is done, its hash is compared with the document's current held text, under the same length test (`sync.send.compared`, with the two hashes, the two texts and where the local one came from); a differing one — an edit made during the upload — asks a pass that sends it. `SyncDifference.between` returns pending sends, pending fetches, conflicts and newly observed agreements. It decides each path once, with no deletion or rename. Sends and fetches put the manifest first, then sorted `data/` paths and sorted `media/` paths; conflicts are sorted by path. A target must supply a document's MD5: a missing or malformed hash is `invalidTransfer`, never agreement inferred from equal lengths.

For a document, *L* is the local hash, *T* the target hash and *S* the last agreed hash. The decision uses those values alone:

| Local and target state | Decision |
|---|---|
| Present locally, absent at the target | Send the local document. |
| Absent locally, present at the target | Fetch a new file without replacing a local name. |
| *L* = *T* | Record that hash as agreed when needed; move no bytes. |
| *L* = *S*, *T* differs | Fetch a replacement carrying the local hash that authorized it. |
| *T* = *S*, *L* differs | Send the local change. |
| Both differ from *S*, or there is no *S* and both are present but different | Report a conflict; send and fetch neither copy. |

Reconciliation samples each film against a service-owned metadata revision captured before either inventory is read. Runner agreements invalidate older samples before their write is queued, including equality, verified sends and receives, and Keep mine. Reading ledger state and committing observed equality are serialized with those writes. A revision change during any metadata wait discards the sampled decision; the existing reconciliation queue coalesces one fresh pass without a polling timer. Once the metadata transaction accepts a difference, each submission checks that its own path has no newer agreement or dismissal and is not being dismissed. An unrelated path completing does not discard the remaining accepted work; a changed path is skipped and gets a fresh pass. An old equality observation therefore cannot replace a newer verified agreement and authorize receiving over a later local edit. This ordering belongs to the one application-owned service; it is not a cross-process transaction over both copies.

A conflict set aside at exactly these two hashes is omitted from the conflict list, while still blocking both directions. A change to either hash raises it again. Equal copies clear an old set-aside entry even when the agreed hash was already correct. Media uses presence and size, with no agreement ledger: a missing local name is fetched, an existing local name is never fetched or replaced, and a differing target size remains a pending send governed by the target's existing-name refusal.

**Agreement storage.** `SyncLedger` records agreed document MD5s and conflicts set aside at their local and target MD5s. `agree`, `agreeAll` and `decide` clear any set-aside entry for the paths they record. A reconciliation's whole ledger transaction is one `decide`: the agreements and asides read once, handed to the pass's decision, and the equal files it found committed in one write in the same serialized step, before jobs are submitted; the pass logs `sync.reconcile.ledger` with the counts read, the asides' paths and the agreements it is about to write (`toAgree`), or `superseded: true` where a newer agreement or dismissal overtook it. The memory implementation keeps data for the run. `inDirectory(directory)` keeps one file directly in that ledger directory, named `<sha256(projectKey + newline + targetId)>.json`. Its JSON contains only `schema: 1`, an `agreed` map of relative document paths to hashes, and an `asides` map of paths to `{mine, theirs}`. The film key and target identity are hashed for the filename and absent from the contents. Paths follow payload safety rules; hashes are 32 lowercase hexadecimal characters. Returned maps cannot mutate the stored state.

Ledger read-modify-write operations serialize per file across store instances in this process. A write flushes a sibling temporary file and renames it into place. A file that cannot be read or parsed, has an unsupported shape, or contains an unsafe path or hash is empty for this run and reported once as `ledgerUnreadable`, with the film's key as the id and the target, the file's name and the error's words. Subsequent agreements may populate that empty state; a reporting callback cannot change its outcome. `SyncService` accepts an optional ledger, defaulting to memory, and uses a separate in-memory ledger for session films even when disk storage was supplied. A verified document send, including a matching-target completion after a lost answer, records its immutable sent hash before its completion can cause another reconcile. It never records a newer local edit as though those bytes were sent. Ledger removal on film deletion or stopping sharing is not provided.

**Receiving contracts and parts.** `SyncTarget.fetch(path, from:)` answers `SyncBytes`: the actual starting offset, whole-file length, whole-file MD5 where known and a byte stream. A target may answer from zero when it cannot honor a requested offset. A file absent under a fetch is retryable `unavailable`, so the next attempt's `find` can establish whether it is gone; an offset outside the file is `invalidTransfer`. The consumer owns and cancels the returned subscription. The folder target streams from the requested offset, reports document MD5s and checks path confinement again when the stream is subscribed; filesystem failures become `unavailable`. The Drive target uses ranged downloads, returns zero when Drive ignores a range, reports its metadata MD5 and holds its client for the receiving stream, including bounded cleanup when nothing listens (§6.10).

`SyncParts` holds media prefixes separately from the film. Its memory factory touches no files; its directory factory stores `<directory>/<film>/<id>.part`, where `film` is the film key's 64-character lowercase SHA-256 and `id` the job's 32-character lowercase hexadecimal identifier. Any other identity is refused as `invalidTransfer` before filesystem access. `held` measures the retained prefix, `append` flushes before completing, `read` starts at the first byte and `discard` removes only that job's part. Operations are ordered per part, so a film's jobs write their parts at once; `keepOnly` is ordered only against that film's other sweeps. A stream error retains and flushes the bytes already appended. `keepOnly` removes only regular files with valid part names for that film whose ids are absent from its keep set; other films, unknown files, directories and links are preserved. Linked film directories and part paths are refused. The service accepts an optional parts store, defaulting to memory, and always gives session films a separate memory store. After a durable film's `engine.restore`, it sweeps that film's parts, retaining fetch jobs in every phase except done, failed and cancelled. Other films' parts are untouched; session restore never invokes the disk sweep. A cancelled or waiting attempt keeps its prefix. Publication normally removes its owned part; a cleanup failure is reported and leaves that part for the ordinary durable-restore sweep, without undoing the verified copy. A permanently failed fetch or the next durable restore after dismissal also removes its owned part.

`SyncLane` names `sync:media` and `sync:data`, with send kinds `sync.media` and `sync.document` and fetch kinds `sync.media.fetch` and `sync.document.fetch`. `laneOf` recognizes all four kinds, `isFetchKind` distinguishes receiving and `isSyncKind` excludes unrelated jobs. `SyncRules.capacities` defaults to four media files and one document. Retry delays start at five seconds and double to fifteen minutes, with a fifth of jitter and no delay beyond that ceiling. The rules also carry the attention threshold of eight failures, a zero settle delay and a progress step of 0.02.

`FolderSyncTarget` implements the target over an existing folder. Its inventory and `find` report the manifest and flat documents with MD5, and flat media with size and no MD5; thumbnails, nested files and symbolic links are excluded. Its inventory resolves the root once and reads each listed file as listed; a file gone since the listing is left out. A missing root is `unavailable` with a retry verdict. `committed` returns zero, so this target restarts incomplete sends from the beginning. Paths are checked before writing and again before publication; traversal and linked destination directories are refused.

A folder send streams into a temporary file, verifies its length and, for a document with an expected MD5, its content, then publishes the complete file. Documents replace their destination by rename, leaving the previous document intact if streaming fails. Media are published with a native hard link that refuses an existing name, including one another program — a teammate's sync client — creates during the send or after the last check; no check-then-rename sequence can overwrite that file. Unsupported hard-link publication is `publishUnsupported` with a permanent verdict. The native call uses SDK `dart:ffi` inside the I/O implementation and adds no package dependency; `dart:io` has no create-only rename or link. A file-system failure is `unavailable` with a retry verdict, carrying the system's words as its `cause`, which every `'$error'` of the sync logs. Incomplete sends leave no new destination and clean their owned temporary part while its parent remains confined; moved or redirected staging and unexpected contents are preserved.

The folder target, local byte stream, ledger store and parts use conditional I/O and stub files (`folder_target*.dart`, `local_bytes*.dart`, `ledger_store*.dart`, `sync_parts*.dart`). `openLocalBytes` streams from `ProjectSource.diskPath` when available, starting at the requested byte offset; otherwise it reads the source's bytes and yields chunks from the same offset. The public barrel compiles for the web, where folder-target operations answer `unavailable` with a permanent verdict.

The payload contract contains schema, required `direction` (`send` or `fetch`), lane, project-relative path, size, MD5, replacement policy, target kind, target identity (`targetId`, a string of 1–128 ASCII letters, digits, underscores or hyphens), failure count and stage. The service writes SHA-256 of the target identity into payload `targetId`, as every queued payload holds it, and runner identity checks compare that hash; the existing engine place key retains the target's original identity. The film key remains solely in the engine record's `projectKey` and `placeKey` metadata, outside the payload. `checkSyncPayload` applies the shared credential word rule and validates allowed fields and types; a path must be a flat project-relative file of its lane. A checkpoint map holds what its target wrote, and only a credential key in it is refused.

A send has stage `queued` or `sending`, may carry `sent` and a checkpoint, requires MD5 and replacement for documents, and has neither for media. A fetch has stage `queued`, `receiving` or `conflict`, may carry `received` between zero and its whole size, and refuses both `sent` and `checkpoint`. Fetch MD5 is required for documents and allowed for media; only a replacing document fetch carries `localMd5`, a 32-character lowercase hash, and its presence makes replacement true. A conflict is a document fetch with that local hash. Send payloads refuse `received` and `localMd5`. A journal payload lacking direction is unsafe: an old send needs attention as `sendFailed`, Retry fails the same check, and Dismiss removes the job rather than migrating it.


**One job per pending file.** `SyncService.reconcile` compares the source and target inventories with their real agreement ledger, commits newly observed equality, and submits fetches, sends, then conflicts to their shared lanes. A conflict is a document-fetch job at stage `conflict`, carrying the current local and target hashes. A closed or loading film queues nothing and retires nothing. An open film without a target retires all its sync jobs the engine holds and queues nothing; a film whose restore failed at opening keeps its journaled jobs, which the engine does not hold, until an opening whose restore succeeds retires them. Reconciles of one film run serially, with overlapping requests sharing one queued follow-up pass. Every duplicate check reads `engine.snapshot`: a media-send or any fetch place key is `[projectKey, 'sync', targetId, path]`, and a document-send place key adds its MD5. Only jobs of the current target block duplicates. A media job in any phase except `done` blocks that path; a live document job blocks the same MD5. A failed, cancelled or attention-requiring document blocks its path regardless of MD5 until dismissed or retried. A fetch or conflict is blocked by any sync job of either direction on its path except a done job; any fetch job except done also blocks a send on that path. Document send identity remains the submission identity when its content changes before sending.

**A job belongs to its target.** `SyncTarget.identity` is opaque and stable: a folder target hashes its canonical root with SHA-256, in hexadecimal; a Drive target uses its folder id. `reconcile` and `restore` retire jobs whose identity differs from the current target, or every job when the open film has no target. Retirement presses Stop where offered, else Dismiss where offered, and does nothing where neither is offered; a job already stopping finishes and is retired at the next pass. Every acknowledgement is checked; refusals report `retireRefused` and do not abort the pass. The runner checks identity at the start and between chunks: another target, or no target while the film is open, returns cancelled. A film that is closed or loading instead waits as `filmClosed`, preserving checkpoints and resuming on reopening.

**The content at send time.** All four sync kinds are interruptible and share a single attempt gate for payload validation, lane hold, open-film binding, target identity and cancellation; none has an engine cleanup runner. Each attempt captures the film's current binding and asks the target's `find` before sending: matching media size or matching current document content settles a lost answer without another put. A missing source file needs attention as `fileGone`; changed media size is `fileChanged`, both with dismissal. A document is read into one immutable snapshot that supplies its current size, MD5 and bytes; changing it discards any checkpoint for older content. The first send checkpoint includes the relative path as `reference`. A saved target checkpoint supplies `committed`, and streaming begins at that offset. The completed receipt and byte count are checked before the job finishes. After a document reaches `done`, its current local hash is compared with the sent hash; only a difference triggers another reconciliation. This settles changes back to a previously queued value without changing place keys or bypassing live-job deduplication.

**Document receiving and conflict choices.** A fetch asks `find` before taking bytes; a missing target file fails as `targetFileGone`, and changed target length or MD5 updates the payload. A document is re-decided from fresh local, target and agreed hashes: the local side is one snapshot — the text the app holds where its length is the file's, else the file's bytes read once and decoded as the source's reader decodes them — whose hash decides and whose text is the guarded write's expected text, logged `sync.fetch.compared {film, id, path, mine, theirs, agreed, from, action}`; a write made since refuses as stale (`sync.fetch.stale {film, id, path, from}`) and the fetch decides again. Equality records agreement and finishes without a write. A target still equal to the agreement finishes and arranges a reconcile after its done view so the local change can be sent. A missing local copy is created, and a local copy still equal to agreement can be replaced. Other divergence checkpoints stage and state code `conflict`, the relative path as reference and `{mine, theirs}` in `stateArgs`, then needs attention with `takeTheirs`, `keepMine` and `dismiss`. Those hashes and the reference survive journal restoration; hashes are never presentation text. A no-copy completion checkpoints only a static `outcome` in `stateArgs`: `alreadyAgreed` when the copies are equal, or `keptLocal` when the local copy is kept, including Keep mine and a target still equal to agreement. The engine preserves that outcome through completion and restoration. Normal receiving checkpoints replace the arguments before a later actual copy, so an earlier outcome cannot describe that copy. These outcomes describe the final decision, not a lifetime receipt history: after a publication interrupted before completion, recovery may find equality and finish already agreed.

`takeTheirs` authorizes a fetch under the fresh local text's guard. `keepMine` records the current target hash as agreed, finishes without a write and arranges a post-completion reconcile to send the local copy. An accepted conflict dismissal records the checkpoint's two hashes as an aside before `act` answers. Dismiss claims the path and invalidates older inventory decisions before its first asynchronous wait. Removing the row and persisting its aside share the film's metadata ordering; the claim remains until that transaction finishes, and is released even on failure. An overlapping reconcile cannot recreate the unchanged conflict in the gap between removal and persistence. Retirement uses the engine's action directly and creates no aside. Take theirs and Keep mine re-decide current contents first: equality or a one-sided change can settle a former conflict without applying an obsolete choice. Dismiss records the displayed pair without reading either side again; its aside suppresses only that exact conflict at a later reconcile.

A document is collected in memory and verified against its whole length and MD5 before strict UTF-8 decoding. Invalid UTF-8 fails as `invalidDocument`; a leading UTF-8 byte-order mark is retained so publication preserves the target bytes. `run.effect` persists intent before `writeText`, using `create: true` for an absent name or `expectedText` for a replacement. A fresh conflict choice is durably consumed before target or local reads. A receiving retry may retain Take theirs only while the local hash still matches the snapshot it approved. A refused stale write durably revokes that choice before repeating the decision, so an intervening edit remains protected even if a later read fails, the job retries or the application restarts. Text guards compare the source representation against the raw snapshot with at most the one leading BOM consumed by the source decoder; an additional leading BOM remains content. A successful guarded write emits one arrival immediately, then records the target hash. If that ledger write fails, the job retains the normal receiving-failure and Retry path; Retry can record observed equality without publishing or announcing the file again. A storage failure after publication therefore does not leave the open film unaware of bytes that already landed. Fetching is a copy through `ProjectSource.writeText` or `writeBytes`, not a person's edit through `ProjectWriter`: its author, viewer and validation rules govern editing. Received files are read like other files on disk, with malformed contents becoming the loader's warning rows.

**Media receiving and verification.** A fetch refuses a local media name as `localNameTaken` before asking for bytes. It resumes at its part's measured length, discarding a part longer than the fresh target size. A response that starts at zero instead of the requested offset discards the old prefix; another unexpected offset is invalid. The retained prefix is hashed from its start; an attempt's incoming chunks go to one `append`, opened at the first chunk and flushed once when the attempt ends, however it ends, and logged as `fetch.part` with the id, path, offset, bytes, outcome (`flushed` or `writeFailed`, with the error's type and text), milliseconds, MB/s and flushes. The reply is read no faster than the part is written, and a failed write ends the fetch at once; checkpoints record `receiving`, the relative reference, `received` and progress at the rules' step. A complete retained part is verified directly, avoiding a request at EOF. The completed length and every available whole-file MD5 must match before `writeBytes` under `run.effect` with `create: true`; a concurrently created local file fails as `localNameTaken` and is preserved. Publication emits one arrival before discarding its part. If only that cleanup fails, the verified publication still completes; `partCleanupFailed` reports the job id and error type without paths or error text. A diagnostic callback cannot change completion. The retained done-job part remains eligible for that film's ordinary durable-restore sweep, which still touches no other film. A length or hash mismatch discards the part and retries as `receivedMismatch` with the usual failure count and attention threshold.

Every returned byte stream is subscribed and cancelled in a `finally`, including rejected metadata, early failure and interruption. Cancellation races a pending stream read, so a stalled transport can release its client. Closing the film is checked at chunk boundaries and waits as `filmClosed`; lane holds, user stops and service shutdown retain incomplete media parts. `SyncService.arrivals` is a broadcast stream of `SyncArrival(projectKey, path)` for successful writes, closed with the service; equality and conflict decisions without a write emit nothing. The service itself does not touch forms or the tree.

`NoSyncService` starts no listener, opens no storage and schedules no work. `restore` and `close` complete without doing anything and `reconcile` returns zero. `jobsFor` returns an empty list; `views` and `arrivals` are empty streams. Every `act` returns a refused acknowledgement with code `unavailable`. These answers let callers use the same service interface when sync is unavailable.

**Arrival refresh.** `SyncAppBinding` listens only for arrivals belonging to the open, ready film. An arrival carries a document's text as a reader of the written file decodes it, a leading byte-order mark consumed as the loader's reader consumes it (`SyncArrival.text`; null for a medium). A text is entered in the write record as it arrives, through `ProjectController.noticeWritten(written:, gesture: 'sync.arrived', publish: false)`, so the record orders it where the folder does: an own save after it wins, and one before it is overwritten by it; the view holds it at once, and the folder watch finds it the app's own. Each arrival restarts a 300 ms quiet interval; its expiry asks one publication for the burst, which lays the texts over the index unread and reads again a path that came without its text. `sync.arrivals {film, paths, landed, read}` and `sync.arrivals.done {film, published, ms}` bracket it. An arrival while a publication runs retains one follow-up burst: if its quiet interval has elapsed when the current one finishes, the next starts then; otherwise its remaining interval is preserved. Publications from arrivals do not overlap. An arrival for another film, a closed film or a loading film is ignored, and changing or closing the film discards its pending burst's publication, its texts staying entered under the source they were written to (`sync.arrivals.dropped {film, paths, entered, reason}`); an arrival for another film or after the binding closed is `sync.arrivals.ignored`, and a burst due while a publication runs is `sync.arrivals.held` until that one ends. A publication in progress uses the controller's own one-at-a-time order. A failed publication reports `sync.refreshFailed {film, paths, type, error, ms}`. The publication's normal project notification reconciles the film; no timer polls a target.

**Progress and recovery data.** Target checkpoint callbacks are validated and copied before their journal writes are queued. Checkpoints passed to `committed` and `put` are separate deep copies, so a target cannot mutate a later journal snapshot through an input map. Every outgoing payload snapshot is validated again. The stream waits for those writes at chunk boundaries before sending more bytes. Progress callbacks update persistence only at `SyncRules.progressStep` intervals, with completion reaching 1.0. Cancellation is checked between chunks and returns `cancelled`; the engine then applies a user stop or lane hold while retaining safe recovery data. A checkpoint that holds a credential key needs attention as `unsafeCheckpoint` and is reported without retaining it.

**Lane holds.** The service's one hold is the sign-in's: a target's sign-in refusal holds its lane as `signInNeeded`, offering only sign-in/stop, until an accepted sign-in action lifts it; a reconciliation never releases it. A hold interrupts sends and fetches in its lane, all interruptible without cleanup runners; targets keep partial recovery bytes private and publish only complete files. A cancelled attempt requeues with its safe checkpoint, so releasing the hold resumes a send from `committed` or a fetch from its part length. A media hold leaves the data lane running, and Stop cancels a held job. A sign-in acknowledgement applies only to the hold it addressed, so an older accepted action cannot erase a newer hold. The service reapplies the hold after an acknowledgement, including a refusal, and checks it again at runner entry before any target effect.

**Failures and reopening.** Retryable target failures increment the persisted failure count, use the rules' exponential delay and need attention with retry/dismiss at the configured threshold. A sign-in failure holds its lane as `signInNeeded` with sign-in/stop actions and answers a retry with zero delay, so every due job immediately exposes that hold; sign-in on one of them lifts the authentication reason. An inventory that FilmOpen Cloud refuses for want of a token — the code `noToken`, with the verdict `signIn` — is reported as `inventoryFailed` and makes every later pass of a `filmopen` target wait for the account's sign-in: the wait, traced once as `reconcile.hold`, is the account's and not a film's, kept in memory for every film at once. A waiting pass asks the cloud nothing and is traced `reconcile.skipped` with the code `signInNeeded`; an ordinary caller gets 0 and a strict one `ReconcileIncomplete('inventory', 'signInNeeded')`. The wait is read after the target is captured, so a film whose target changed to a folder or Drive is listed and one whose target was removed answers `noTarget`. `SyncService.accountSignedIn()` ends it, traced `reconcile.release`; the application calls it through `SyncAppBinding.accountReady()` whenever the account reports a user — a sign-in or a restored session, never the saved hint alone — and reconciles the open film with the reason `signedIn`, logged `sync.binding.accountReady`; a film closed then reconciles when it opens. A restart starts clear. Drive's refusal and a Cloud `401` keep the code `refused` and hold nothing: each pass asks again. The account's sign-in does not lift a lane's `signInNeeded` hold. Permanent target failures become `failed`. Unexpected failures become `sendFailed` or `receiveFailed` for their direction, with retry/dismiss and report the error's text as `attemptFailed`. An unavailable binding waits as `filmClosed` with resume/stop. Binding availability is rechecked around local reads and between stream chunks; closing a film interrupts the send while preserving its checkpoint for reopening. The locally detected closure is retained even if the target translates the stream error. Opening while an interrupted attempt drains also observes its eventual filmClosed result and resumes it while the film is open. `restore` binds all four durable sync kinds by the film key, resumes that film's `filmClosed` jobs and reconciles; a session restore skips the disk journal. `jobsFor` filters by film and sync kind, `views` exposes sync jobs, and `close` closes the owned engine and arrivals stream. `SyncService.current` starts as `NoSyncService` until the application installs a service.

The service's `JobReport` callback receives `inventoryFailed` with an empty id and the failure's text for failed inventory work — a target's failure reads `SyncTargetException: <code> (<verdict>)`, as every logged sync error does — `attemptFailed` with the job id and the error's text for unexpected transfers, and `unsafeCheckpoint` with the job id and the refused checkpoint's keys and refusal, never its values, where a credential would be. The text is the error's as it is, its message and any path in it included; a diagnostic callback that throws cannot change a transfer's outcome.

**The trace.** `SyncService(trace:)` takes a `SyncTrace`, `void Function(String event, Map<String, Object?> data)`; `createApplicationSyncService` installs one that writes each event to the log at info as `sync.<event>`. A trace that throws is ignored. A line carries job ids, lanes, kinds, codes, verdicts, reasons, stages, error types, counts, sizes, attempts and milliseconds; the film's key as `film`; a file by its project-relative `path`, with its area (`data`, `media` or `root`) where it matters; the target by its kind and its identity as it is where a pass starts, a folder's root beside it; a conflict's two whole hashes; and a failure's own text as `error`, as it is — a target's failure reads `SyncTargetException: <code> (<verdict>)`. It never carries a token: a request is logged before its token is asked.

Every reconcile line carries `film` but `reconcile.release`, which is the account's. A pass that reaches its film logs `reconcile.retire {reason, jobs}` (the film's sync jobs it looks through) before stopping the jobs of any previous target, then `reconcile.start {kind, identity, root, reason}` (the target's identity as it is, and for a folder, whose identity is a digest of its root, the root) (`open` from `restore`, else the caller's: `change`, `formLeft`, `formMoved`, `writesSettled`, `released`, `background`, `foreground`, `signedIn`, `flush`, or the service's own `changedDuringSend` and `fetchDecided`), `reconcile.inventory {local, target, ms}`, `reconcile.decided {sends, fetches, conflicts, agreed, deferred, ms}` (`deferred`: the document sends held for an open form) and `reconcile.done {queued, ms}`; a pass that does nothing logs `reconcile.skipped {reason, code: serviceClosed | filmClosed | noTarget | signInNeeded | superseded, ms}`, and one that fails `reconcile.failed {stage: retire | local | inventory | decide | submit, type, ms}` (`retire` while the jobs of a previous target are stopped) — a refused submission still propagating as before. Each job a pass stops or dismisses there is traced as the action is sent, `retire {id, kind, phase, state, action, film, reason: targetChanged}`, and as it ends, `retire.done {id, action, outcome: accepted | refused, code, ms}`. FilmOpen Cloud's missing token logs `reconcile.hold {film, reason: noToken}` once, and the account's sign-in `reconcile.release {reason: signIn}`, with no film. A pass asked of a closed service logs `reconcile.skipped` with `serviceClosed`. The direction is the runner's, and a count is traced only where it is an integer, since a rejected payload's may be anything. An attempt logs `send.start` or `fetch.start {id, lane, area, path, size, from, attempt}` once its gates pass — a held, closed or retargeted attempt logs none, the last ending `.cancelled` —, `send.checkpoint {id, sent, progress}` at each saved progress step, `fetch.decided {id, outcome: alreadyAgreed | keptLocal | conflict | receive}`, and one terminal line read from its result: `.done {id, size, ms}`, `send.settled {id, reason: alreadyThere, ms}`, `.cancelled {id, sent | received, ms}`, `.waiting {id, reason: filmClosed | formOpen | hold, code, ms}` (a lane hold, a sign-in's included), `conflict {id, area, path, mine, theirs, ms}` (the two whole MD5s), or `.failed {id, stage, code, type, verdict, failures, nextMs, ms}`. A failure's stage is where the attempt was: `read` (the local file), `target`, `write` (publishing a received file) or `agree` (the agreement ledger); a media send's bytes are read inside the target's `put` and count as `target`, the error's type telling the two apart. `verdict` is `retry` where a retry is scheduled, else the phase, `needsAttention` or `failed`; `type` is present only for an exception; `nextMs` is the retry delay the result carries, present only where a retry is scheduled. `action {id, action, accepted, code, phase}` records every action on a sync job — a Stop of a queued or held job, which no runner sees, among them — and `conflict.resolved {id, action}` an accepted choice on a conflict; `hold {lane, code, reason: signIn}` and `release {lane}` mark a lane's hold changing; `restore {film, jobs, waiting, ms}` and `restore.failed {film, stage, type, ms}` end a restore, and `restore.skipped {film, code: serviceClosed, ms}` one asked of a closed service, which activates nothing. `send.woken {film, id, reason: formLeft | restore, ms}` marks a `formOpen` wait resumed by the service itself, `send.held {film, id, reason: formOpen}` one a restore leaves waiting, and `deferral.failed {film, path, type}` an application answer that threw, which holds the send. A job's one follow-up — a document send's compare (`sendCompare`), a fetch decision's pass (`fetchDecision`), a form's wake (`formOpenWake`), a closed attempt's wake (`closedAttemptWake`) — waits through one helper for the job to leave `working`, logged `job.settled {film, id, for, phase, code, ms}` before it acts, `job.settled {…, dropped: serviceClosed | jobGone}` where the wait ends without acting, and `job.settled {…, failed, type, error}` beside the report where the follow-up fails.

Folder sends stage under the captured canonical target root, outside the media and data directories. Redirected staging parents are refused before pathname operations; cleanup removes only the owned part and an empty temporary directory, never unexpected contents recursively. These pathname checks do not isolate the process from a hostile local program with permission to replace directories between a check and a filesystem call.

**A document whose form is open is sent when the person leaves it** — what they typed; a change that was not typing goes at once (below). `SyncService(deferSend:)` takes a `SyncDeferral`, `bool Function(String projectKey, String path)`; null never defers, and an answer that throws holds the send. It is asked of every send in the data lane — the documents and the manifest — and never of media or a fetch; the application's answer holds only flat `data/` documents, so the manifest never waits. At submission a held document is not queued — the question asked at each submission itself, after every earlier one's wait, a document found held only then logged `send.deferred {film, area, path, reason: formOpen, ms}` — and `reconcile.decided` counts what the decision found held as `deferred`. Before each attempt asks the target, and again immediately before it reads the file's bytes — whether the job was queued, restored from the journal or retrying after a failure — a held send answers `waiting('formOpen')` with resume and stop, so no attempt reads bytes that are still being typed. An attempt that answered `formOpen` after its form was left is resumed once its wait is committed, and `restore` resumes a persisted `formOpen` wait whose form is not open — after a crash, none is. `reconcile(projectKey, reason:, settle:, strict:)` keeps its zero for every ordinary caller; `settle: false` skips the rules' settle delay, and `strict: true` throws `ReconcileIncomplete(stage: inventory | superseded, code)` where the target could not be listed or a newer pass superseded this one, each coalesced caller getting its own outcome.

`createApplicationSyncService` answers the question through the container's `SyncAppBinding` (`syncAppBindingProvider`), as `documentSendWaits`: a flat `data/<stem>.json` waits while a form is open on that stem in that film (`OpenDrafts.of(stem, projectKey:)`) unless the binding lifted the deferral for the film, and every document waits while any draft's write is in flight (`OpenDrafts.writesPending`), so a send queued behind a form's last write never reads the bytes from before it. The binding hears `OpenDrafts.changes`: a form removed (`formLeft`) or moved to another file (`formMoved`) in the open film resumes that film's `formOpen` waits no form holds and reconciles, logged `sync.deferral.lifted {film, reason, resumed, held, ms}` (`held`: the waits an open form or a write in flight still holds; `reason` is also `background` and `leaving`); `OpenDrafts.writesSettled`, the last tracked write ending, does the same with reason `writesSettled`, a document whose own form is still open staying held. A film without a target logs `sync.wake.skipped {film, reason, code: notSynced | noFilm, ms}` and wakes nothing; a wake that fails logs `sync.wake.failed {film, reason, error, ms}`, a job whose place cannot be read `sync.deferral.unplaced {film, id}`, and a refused resume `sync.deferral.resumeRefused {film, id, code}`. A `signInNeeded` hold is never woken: only `formOpen` waits are resumed. The app's lifecycle reaches the binding through the window's one `AppLifecycleListener` (`lifecycleChanged`): leaving the foreground — `inactive`, `hidden` or `paused`, coalesced into one — settles the drafts and, once they are written, lifts the deferral for every film, resumes the open film's `formOpen` waits and reconciles (`background`), so a phone put away sends what its form held; returning (`resumed`) restores the deferral and reconciles once (`foreground`, logged `sync.deferral.restored {film, reason: foreground, lifted, ms}`), so an idle desktop picks up another device's edit. Leaving starts with `sync.background.start {film, state, ms}`; a settle that throws logs `sync.background.settleFailed {error}`, and drafts that could not be written keep the deferral (`sync.background.refused {film, code: draftsNotSettled, ms}`); a return before the settle ends, or the binding's close, lifts nothing (`sync.background.skipped {code: returned | closed, ms}`). There is no polling and no timer. When the preparation ends — settle, lift and reconcile, a refused settle or a pass with nothing to send included — `SyncAppBinding.backgroundPrepared`, a synchronous `Stream<void>`, fires only if that preparation is still the current one: its lifecycle ticket current, the binding open and still away. An obsolete or closed preparation does not fire, so a notice never crosses into a later cycle; each end logs `sync.background.prepared {outcome: emitted | suppressed, reason: current | superseded | returned | closed, ms}`.

**A change that was not typing goes at once.** A render's or an import's insertion, a child added or moved — every change through `ProjectController.patchEntity` — is sent as soon as it is written, even while its form stays open; through an open form the text written carries what the form held, typed or not. Where the patch wrote its change and a full re-read proved it and still holds exactly the text its own write left, it hands the binding that text (`SyncAppBinding.release(projectKey, stem, text, ms:)`, in memory only), logged `sync.deferral.released {film, path, source: patch, ms}`, and the binding wakes the film (reason `released`): a `formOpen` wait resumes and a reconcile submits the document, which goes in its turn behind any other. `deferSend` lifts a released document while the open film's view (§7.5) holds exactly the text released; a write in flight still holds every document, as above. The release ends before every later write of that document through the controller — the form's save (`saveEntity`), the patch writer and the epoch declaration on the viewer's own project file — logged `sync.deferral.releaseDropped {film, path, reason: written, ms}` (`ms`: the release's age), so what the person types afterwards waits for the form again; it ends too where the view's text differs (`textChanged`), another film opens (`filmChanged`), or the film closes or loads or the binding closes (`closed`). A patch that wrote nothing (`unchanged`), whose callback did not make its change (`rejected`), whose write was refused or not proved (`refused`), whose re-read found a later save in place of its own text — the person typed meanwhile, which waits for the form (`overtaken`) — or whose film has no target (`noTarget`) releases nothing, logged `sync.deferral.releaseSkipped {film, path, reason, ms}`. The patch itself logs `entity.patch.start {stem, form}`, `entity.patch.done {stem, wrote, ms}`, a write's failure `entity.patch.failed {stem, stage: write, code, type, ms}` and a refused result `entity.patch.refused {stem, stage: bind | verify | patch, code, type, ms}` — the stem as its digest, never the refusal's text or stack. A release lost to a restart leaves the document to the ordinary rules.

**Leaving a film, in one budget.** `SyncAppBinding.prepareLeaving(projectKey, within:)` settles the drafts, lifts the film's deferral, resumes its `formOpen` waits, reconciles at once (`flush`, `settle: false`, `strict: true`) and waits for the film's document sends — media went when written — until none is queued or working, or `within` runs out; a send waiting to retry is pending, not waited for, since its retry may be minutes away. Each stage takes what is left of the one budget and checks it, and whether the leaving was undone, before it acts — the resume of each `formOpen` wait included — so a late settle never lifts; a lift that throws is that stage's failure, the deferral kept, and an undo during the wait answers `incomplete` with `code: undone`. It answers `LeavingResult {outcome, stage, sent, pending, code, ms}`: `complete` where every document send is done or known pending in the journal, `incomplete` where the inventory failed, the pass was superseded, the leaving was undone or the budget ran out before the sends were known, `refused` where the drafts could not be written. A film without a target ends `complete` at `lift` with `code: notSynced`, nothing lifted; a budget run out at `wait` ends `complete` with `code: timeout`, the sends still under way counted pending. A pending send keeps its journal entry and goes at the next open. `undoLeaving(projectKey)` restores the deferral (`sync.deferral.restored {reason: stayed}`); opening another film, closing it and the binding's own close clear every lift. It begins with `sync.leaving.begin {film, withinMs}`; each stage logs `sync.leaving.start {film, stage, leftMs}` and `sync.leaving {film, stage, outcome, code, ms}`; the end is `sync.flush {film, outcome, stage, sent, pending, code, ms}`, with `sync.leaving.timedOut {film, stage, pending, ms}` where the budget ran out. `ProjectController` calls it on *Close project*, before another folder or the sample opens (a superseded open undoes it, unless a newer open is already preparing the same leaving), and, for a project created or imported, once the new folder has been read whole and before it replaces the film — each after the drafts were settled and within what remains of `kSettleBeforeLeaving` (close's own first settle bounded by it too). A `refused` leaving stops a switch, a creation's or an import's opening as a refused open does: the film and its form stay, the preparation is undone, a folder already created staying on disk; a preparation that throws is undone and logged `project.leavingFailed {type}`; *Close project* goes on whatever the leaving answers. The window's exit (`DraftsBeforeExit`) prepares the open film first and then runs `ExitTasks.runAll` — the sync's own close among them — with what remains of its 10 s — a preparation that throws logged `app.exit.failed {stage, type, ms}` and the exit going on — logged `app.exit.start {stage: prepare, synced}` and `app.exit {stage: prepare | tasks, outcome, pending, ms}`.

Two predicates read the existing jobs and drafts and keep no state of their own, both false outside the binding's open, ready film with a target: `transferring(projectKey)` — a sync job queued, working or waiting for a retry (a wait whose code is not `formOpen`, `filmClosed`, `signInNeeded` or `paused`) — and `unsent(projectKey)` — `transferring`, or a job needing attention or waiting for any reason, or a form open on one of the film's files (`OpenDrafts.openIn`).

**Media on demand.** `SyncService(mediaOnDemand:)` takes a `MediaOnDemand` answer per film; null fetches every remote-only file as before; an answer that throws fails that reconcile pass at its boundary (`reconcile.failed {film, stage: onDemand, type, ms}`, a strict caller hearing `ReconcileIncomplete`) and never chooses eager downloading, and `createApplicationSyncService` answers true for every film, so on a device that pulled a project the documents arrive at once and the media stay in the cloud until something reads them. Under the answer, `SyncDifference.between` lists a `media/` file the target holds and the device does not as **available** (`AvailableMedia`: path and size) instead of fetching it: an available file is never sent, deleted, agreed, journaled or a conflict, and documents, local sends, size mismatches and conflicts decide as before. The service keeps each film's available paths with the target they were listed at, published in the decision's order, readable at once through `availableIn(projectKey)` and replayed to every new listener of `availability` (`SyncAvailability {projectKey, media}`) before its changes; a film with no target, or listed at another target, is cleared. A media fetch's publication invalidates the film's decision state as a document's agreement does, so an inventory sampled before it cannot republish the file, and removes the file from the snapshot. `fetchNow(projectKey, path, reason:)` is the one door: inside the film's decision order it rechecks the film, its target and the snapshot, joins a fetch job for the path that is not done, failed or cancelled (answering its id), else submits the ordinary media fetch (`replace: false`) and answers the new id; not open, no target, another target or not available answers null. Lines: `reconcile.decided {…, available}`, `availability {film, count}`, `fetch.asked {film, reason, path}` before the decision order is entered, `fetch.submit {film, path, ms}` before the submission, `fetch.requested {film, reason, area: media, path, size, joined, id, ms}`, `fetch.refused {film, reason: filmClosed | noTarget | notAvailable | targetChanged, path, ms}`, `fetch.failed {film, stage: submit, path, type, ms}` — each path as it is. `NoSyncService` has no available media and fetches nothing.

**The status, every file against its target.** At each pass that decides, beside the available media, the service keeps the film's `SyncStatus {projectKey, at, files}` (`sync_status.dart`): every path of the local and the target inventories that syncs, in path order, each a `SyncFile {path, state, progress, code}` in the decision's word — `conflict`, `toSend` (here only, or changed here), `toFetch` (there only, or changed there), `available` — else, for a document whose two copies differ (a conflict the person set aside), still `conflict`, and otherwise `same`; `at` is the environment's `now`. It is cleared, as the available media are, where the film has no target or is listed at another one. `statusOf(projectKey)` answers it with the film's jobs laid over it (`SyncStatus.withJobs`): a file whose transfer is queued or working is `moving` with its progress, a waiting one `waiting`, a conflict job `conflict` and any other failed or attention one `problem`, each with its job's state code, one finished since the comparison `same`; a cancelled one, one already finished when the comparison was made (the status keeps those jobs' ids) and *Keep mine*'s (`outcome: keptLocal`, whose send follows) keep the decision's word; where a file has several jobs the one moving wins, then a problem, a wait, a finished one. A finished transfer dismissed before the next pass keeps its result in the status (`status.settled {film, path, id, reason: dismissed}`), so its file does not read as to send again. `statusChanges` names each film whose status was made, settled or cleared, at once; null and nothing where no pass has compared the film. No call to the target is made for it: it is the inventories the pass already read. Lines: `status {film, at, total, counts, files: {path: state}, ms}`, `status.cleared {film, reason: noTarget | targetChanged}`. `NoSyncService` has compared nothing. `filmopen_state`'s `syncStatusProvider(projectKey)` reads `statusOf` at once, at each `statusChanges` of that film and at each change of the jobs, equal snapshots suppressed.

`filmopen_state`'s `MediaAvailability` is the application's `MediaFetch` (§6.9), installed by `createApplicationSyncService` with its log (`media.onDemand.installed {policy}`): it answers `isRemote` from the sync's retained snapshot (`availableIn`), so a consumer built at any moment needs no later turn, and hears `availability` only to tell the widgets what changed. `ensure(source, path)` decides from the consumer's own film alone: the file in that source (`fileLength`) → `local` at once, whatever the last announcement said; else available in that film's snapshot → `fetchNow`, following that job by id — queued, waiting for a retry, working with its progress — until it is done (`fetched`) or has stopped (`failed` with its state code); neither local nor available, or a refused `fetchNow`, is `notAvailable`. A read begun in a film that is no longer bound is refused, never turned towards the open film. A local file is answered with no line, every open of one passing here; otherwise it logs `media.ensure.start {film, path, reason}`, `media.ensure.decided {film, path, decision: fetch | notAvailable, ms}`, `media.ensure.step {film, id, phase, code, ms}` at each change of phase and `media.ensure.done {film, path, outcome, code, id, ms}` — a source that cannot be measured is `media.ensure.failed {…, stage: local, …}` and the code `localUnreadable`, a `fetchNow` that throws `media.ensure.failed {film, path, stage: request, error, ms}` and the code `requestFailed` — and publishes each file's `MediaFetchState`. Previews never download: the media tile (§9.3), the epoch card and the outfit row (`epochPictureFor`; a take is always local, being found in the device's own listing) and a mention's picture (`ProjectMentionCandidates`) paint the stock picture for a remote file. The upload's own read of local bytes is the transfer itself and asks for nothing.

**A scene's media before it plays or is exported** (§6.14, §19.15). `SceneMedia.ready(stem, reason:)` (`sceneMediaProvider`) takes the files the scene's timeline is made of (`SceneTimeline.inputsOf`'s `present`), keeps those not in the media folder that the sync holds on its target (`isRemote`), and asks for them all at once through `ensure` — the sync's media lane runs a few at a time —, saying each one's end to its caller (done, total); a medium no scene being played uses stays on the target. When every one is here the scene's timeline is built again at once (`sceneTimelineProvider` invalidated for that scene, not left to the project's next re-read), and that timeline is the answer, so that what plays or is exported holds the files just fetched; a failure answers the first failed file and its code, and no timeline. Nothing is fetched where nothing is remote or the seam is not the sync's: the timeline held is the answer — asked again where the seam is the sync's, so that a medium another reader made local before the project's re-read is built in. It logs `scene.media.start {scene, reason}`, then `scene.media.none {why: no_sync | no_project | no_scene | no_store | nothing_remote, files, ms}` or `scene.media.fetch {film, files, ms}`, each file's `scene.media.file {file, outcome, code, ms}` (its size is in the sync's `fetch.requested`), and `scene.media.done {fetched, revision, ms}` or `scene.media.failed {file, code, failed, ms}`; a scene whose inputs or timeline cannot be read logs `scene.media.failed {stage: inputs | build, error, ms}` and throws.

**The transfer host.** On Android, uploads and downloads in hand when the person leaves the app go on under a data-sync foreground service until they are done; nothing is shown while the app is in front. `filmopen_platform`'s `BackgroundTransfers` (`show`, `update`, `hide`, `events`) is the seam, in threes: Android answers through the activity's channel `filmopen/sync_host`; every other platform has none (`supported` false, every request `HostStatus.notSupported`); a refusal or a missing handler answers `HostStatus.failed` with its code, never a silent no-op. Nothing touches the channel before the first `show`. `filmopen_state`'s `SyncHost`, made in `lib/main.dart` beside the binding and hearing the lifecycle before it, shows the host at the first departure (`inactive`, the activity still visible) when the open film is `unsent`, holds it until `backgroundPrepared`, then follows the film's moving jobs — counted as `transferring` counts them, the mean of their progress, an unknown progress as zero — with an update for a change of count or a step of 5 %, and hides it when nothing is `transferring`, on `resumed`, and on the platform's `timeout` (Android 15's daily data-sync allowance). Where no job is counted the notification says *FilmOpen is finishing your changes*, its bar moving. The texts are resolved from the state's localisation and travel over the channel. The activity's answer to `show` or `hide` is the request accepted, not its result: the service reports its own start (`started {ms}`), its failure to start (`failed {stage: notification | startForeground, type, message, ms}`, the exception's type and its message, and the service ends), its timeout and every stop with its reason (`hidden`, `timeout`, `failed`, `destroyed` by the system) and how long it ran; the activity's destroy reports `stopped {reason: activityDestroyed, ms}` before it stops the service and clears its reporter, though its engine ending at once may keep that line from the app's log. Logcat gets the same codes and times, and a failure's exception message, and is the dependable record of an activity's destroy. A failed start is not retried until the next departure; a platform without a host logs `sync.host.unsupported {platform}` once and does nothing else. The trail: `sync.host.idle {reason: noFilm | nothingUnsent, state}`, `sync.host.requested {action: show | update | hide, transferring, unsent, reason: inactive | count | progress | empty | resumed | timeout, files}` (a request, not proof), `sync.host.accepted {action: show | hide, ms}`, `sync.host.started {files, ms}` and `sync.host.stopped {reason, ms}` from the service's reports, `sync.host.released {shown, ms}`, `sync.host.updated {files, progress (a whole percent, a step of 5 compared in whole points), ms}`, `sync.host.failed {stage, code, message?, ms}` — `start | update | stop` for a request the channel refused (its message on `sync.host.callFailed`), `notification | startForeground` for the service's own failure, with the exception's `message` —, `sync.host.timeout {files, ms}`; never a path or a name, and no text but an exception's message. The service stops with the activity (its engine is the activity's), so a swipe-away stops the transfers and the journal resumes them at the next open (§14).

**Application ownership.** After constructing insertion, `lib/main.dart` calls `createApplicationSyncService`, assigns `SyncService.current` and constructs `SyncAppBinding`. The journal directory is `syncDirectory()` where application files are available, otherwise null. Its `targetFor` callback uses Sharing's `sharingTargetFor(project, auth: container.read(driveAccountProvider).auth, folderPathFor: container.read(settingsProvider.notifier).sharingFolderFor)`: Drive configuration comes through Sharing, while folder paths are machine preferences keyed by the film. The root directly depends on `filmopen_sync` for service ownership and reaches Drive only through `filmopen_sharing`. Construction is inert: it reads no credentials and sends no network request. The binding restores a real film's jobs and reconciles its changes — a Sharing gesture's manifest write among them, which the gesture does not reconcile again (§9.4); the web and session copies have no sharing target. A construction failure logs `sync.unavailable` with the error type alone, and the no-op service remains available. Shutdown runs through the binding's `ExitTasks` registration.

### 6.15 Renders — `filmopen_render`, `filmopen_render_ui`

The model picker lists model/platform routes under nonselectable platform headers, ordered by platform name and then model name. Each concrete plug-in and destination retains its identity. Unavailable rows are grey and show their route's reason. An installed route's unavailable row links to Settings → Plugins. A catalogue model with no backend candidate remains visible with a missing-plug-in reason; an installed route excluded by the place's input rules stays excluded. The place's media-kind filter still applies. Where different offered models share a catalogue name, their menu rows and selected values add a localized input mode: from references for a model accepting `reference_images`, from a prompt for one that does not. A model with no offered namesake retains its name; route labels and identities remain unchanged.

**A render is asked for once and frozen.** `RenderRequest` holds what the person chose when they pressed *Render*
— the model, the route (the plug-in, its platform, the id of the key and the plug-in's way of running the model),
the destination where a route has a choice of servers (`RenderDestination`, `local`, `server` or `lent`: the application's
name for it and a server provider's reference, never an address), the prompt composed once, the options by the
catalogue's parameter names (a negative prompt is one of them, `negative_prompt`, as the interface has no other
door for it), the seed, from 0 to 4294967295, the film's media by input name, the quote (`RenderQuote`: a cost with
its own basis, what one cost buys and the service's name for the quote, or `unknown` and no cost at all), the place
its outputs go as the insertion writes an `InsertionTarget` — which always has a project and a stem, and is read
here for nothing else — the treatment that applied when the person asked, as the dialog froze it (`RenderTreatment`,
the shape its batch item keeps, Project Specification §8.7, §12.2; §9.3), the stage the dialog chose — starting at the
version's `render_stage`, `prototype` where it says none or one of another shape — or, for the render commands, the
version's, the purpose and the origin: `local`, `developer` — asked for from the developer page, of the plug-in open
there, which the host runs as the page's working copy only while the page waits for it (§6.11, §9.4a) — or the
reserved `remote`. Its JSON is a job's payload: no credential, no address, and the key's id written `key_id`, which
the jobs' journal does not take for a credential (§6.12); a JSON that is not a whole request reads as none. What it
holds is copied and frozen all the way down, so nothing said afterwards — a dialog closed, a setting changed,
another film opened — changes it.

An outfit's `outfit_sheets` Render door selects the composite in render UI; every other door retains its one-source behavior. The dialog shows the Prompt card on the outfit's own `prompts.sheet` — the prompt its page's card edits, writers' target `outfit-sheet`, its `@` offering the outfit's inspirations and references — bound to the outfit's open form's draft where one is open, else read-only. **The outfit's own words carry the sheet** ("this character, as shown, wearing this outfit"): the character's words are neither sent nor read for tokens. **The character is frozen as the dialog opens**: its open form's draft, including typing, else the indexed file, at the exact filename epoch — the SHA-256 of its complete canonical JSON document and its live references. **The outfit is taken at *Render***, from the one document the card shows (the open draft as it stands, else the file as the dialog opened on it): its words, the media they name and the SHA-256 of that document, so typing or Generate in the card never refuses its own render. The negative is the outfit's own; the existing visual treatment is applied once after the prompt. Submission rechecks the project opening, viewer, fresh exact-epoch parent pick and both hashes — the character's against the film now, the outfit's captured document against its draft or file now — before returning a request, and logs `render.outfit.sources {captured: opening|render, parent: frozen, outcome: ok|refused, reason?: project|closing|viewer|parentPick|missing|changed, ms}` at the character's capture and at that check (the source documents and references logged whole). It refuses when the captured target's project key differs from the sources' key (`project`), and as soon as the captured project is no longer the open one — another requested, including while the opening waits for drafts to settle, closed or replaced (`closing`). A changed binding or source refuses inline with the existing stale action sentence; an outfit file changed on disk with no form open is refused so, never sent with its old words under a new hash; an initially unresolved parent refuses with the existing unresolved sentence. The final prompt, including the one treatment application, and the snapshots are frozen in the request. A take continued at an outfit's sheet keeps its box and sends its words whole, as recorded or edited; its sources are checked as a new render's.

Besides the pictures a prompt names (§9.8) and a continued take's, only this composite context makes `reference_images` an available input for model eligibility. It is one ordered list, each picture once: the readable live entries of the character's reference group, frozen as the dialog opens, first; then the pictures the outfit's words name, or, where they name none, the outfit's own live references, read at *Render* — each group's order kept, archived and deleted entries excluded, with kept and unreviewed entries both usable. A model accepting that input receives the list actually sent, and the dialog refuses inline below its declared `min` or above its `max` of that list, stating the count without truncation or a new selection control, logged `render.outfit.bound {outcome: ok|refused, reason?: referencesBelow|referencesAbove, count, min?, max?, references, ms}`, decided once each time the model or the pictures change, never per frame; `render.outfit.inputs {from: named|composite|none, character, outfit, sent, words: own, bound, references, entries: [{reference, unreviewed, favorite}], skipped: [{reference, reason}], ms}` (the pictures sent; a line is said again where they change, at the same count too) says which, with each part's count, where it changes. A model accepting no references receives none (`from: none`), and the dialog says it renders from the prompt alone. **The outfit's tokens resolve in the outfit** (`renderOutfitPromptInputs`), against its `outfit-sheet` candidates, and are written in the row's placeholders by their number in that one list — after the character's pictures, so a parent portrait and the outfit's `@reference-1` send the portrait first and write the token as the second picture; the whole list is held to the model's bound, never cut (`references-count`), and a token naming nothing refuses `tokens-missing`, as every prompt's does. The kind at an outfit's sheets is `i2i` where the outfit's words name a picture, both modes where they name none, and follows the card's words as they change (§9.8).

**Source composition freezes provenance.** `SourceSnapshot` derives its entity identity, exact filename epoch and picked version number from the complete picked version stem. A path, extension or pointer is not that stem. Each primary or dependency snapshot retains a caller-supplied `ContentHash` with its algorithm named; the render package computes no digest and performs no source resolution while constructing a snapshot. `RenderComposition` freezes the dependency list, prompt and structured reference strings and maps in the same recursive JSON shape as `RenderRequest.inputs`. The source passed to the request remains the complete primary picked version stem, not its logical entity identity. Both source hashes and the selected references remain captured when the render is submitted.

A one-source request needs no composer: its prompt, source and inputs go into the request as they are. The UI consumer belongs in `filmopen_render_ui`. `OutfitRenderComposer` resolves the parent character by removing the outfit tag's last dotted segment (Project Specification §9.2), reading only the character group's exact filename epoch and using `FilmProject.pickAt` for the viewer. Missing or ambiguous parents remain unresolved, with no epoch fallback or index mutation. It joins the character's positive prompt it is given before the outfit's with a blank line, omitting an empty side — the dialog gives it none, the outfit's words carrying the sheet —, and preserves the character references before the outfit references without truncation, the dialog giving as the outfit's only those the character does not hold already. Its dependency must name that exact-epoch parent. State, forms, browser and tree do not reach render through a resolver. Optional request provenance is `primary_snapshot` and `source_dependencies`, each snapshot holding `stem` and `content_hash` with its named algorithm and value. The primary stem equals the request's source; dependencies require a primary. The existing job and journal codecs retain these fields at submission and restore, refuse malformed provenance, and omit it entirely from ordinary one-source requests.

**One queue, which needs no film.** `RenderManager` holds every render: `submit` never refuses because a queue is
busy, and answers the render's id — the jobs engine's own where the manager runs on it, else a random UUID
(`newRenderJobId`) — which `ctx.job.id` carries, in the form both the jobs' journal takes for a job's id and the
insertion keeps as a producer's recovery (§6.13): 8 to 96 lowercase hexadecimal digits and dashes, the first a digit
(`isRenderJobId`); a queue per `RenderResource` — a server or a key's
account, named by its platform and a digest of what stands behind it and never by an address, which the backend
says for each request — runs as many as its capacity, the
person's number, else its platform plug-in's, else 1 on the person's own machine and 2 on a key, a number below 1
being none (`RenderCapacity`), never more than three in all; `stop` keeps a queued render from starting, ends a call
in flight, asks the service to stop work it holds, collecting what it had finished, and gives up staged outputs
nobody took in, letting them go while what they cost stays recorded — each then cancelled; `pokeNow(id)` asks about held work at once instead of at its next poll — a lending notice said the job changed (§6.6c) — cancelling the poll's timer and sharing a question already in flight; held as `restore` reads it, so ready outputs and a render waiting for a person ask nothing, nor does an unknown job; its decision said before it waits, `render.poke.start {found, branch: collect|shared|skipped}`, and its end by `render.poke {found, inFlight, reason: collected|shared|notHeld|unknown, ms}`; `restore` keeps a queued
render queued, asks again about a pending one, makes one whose outputs were staged ready again and marks a single
call cut off by a closing application `interrupted`, sent nowhere, and the application runs it before the
insertion's own, so that a producer's run finds its job; nothing is sent twice by itself. Its refusals are
`RenderRefused` codes. An incomplete journal reading refuses new managed submissions as `journal`, preserving
work whose state or resource this build could not read. `RenderManager.current` is `NoRenderManager` until the application installs one.

**The manager the application runs**, `BackendRenderManager`, over a backend and a `RenderJournal`: every render in
the order asked, each resource as many at a time as its capacity and three in all; its id a `newRenderJobId`; its
intent written before its call — the journal holds `submitting` before anything is sent, so a restart that finds it
there calls it `interrupted` — and **nothing sent where the intent was not written**, the render then `failed` with
the code `journal`, nor where a stop or a close came while it was; the whole document after every change, the
renders not over and the newest hundred completed results, in memory as in the file (`RenderJob.toJson`, read back by
`fromJson`); a private durable terminalHistory orders retention by completion, separately from public reverse submission order. A newly acknowledged older result therefore remains confirmable through unrelated saves and restart until later completions age it out. Legacy journals seed retention from their existing order; changing the retained limit applies a smaller bound without treating a valid older larger history as corruption. Unreadable or contradictory retention ranks are discarded while valid known terminal ranks and the ordinary fallback order are retained; work a service holds asked about again at its plug-in's hint, kept between 1 and 60 seconds, one
question at a time, holding its slot meanwhile; acknowledgement and Stop persist their disposition before letting
anything go; **a stop acts on what the call it waited for answered** — work a
service now holds is cancelled by its new receipt, work that is over, made or failed — a render whose service had
finished and whose downloads the stop cut among it (`RenderFailed` `cancelled` at the stage `downloading`) — is
not cancelled at all, and nothing is fetched again.
An end is written twice: its prepared payload before settlement, and its applied end, the candidate still in the journal, after it, before publication or releasing capacity; a refused write keeps the candidate and resource in settling with Retry alone. The next write, or a restore, prunes the ends retention no longer keeps. Retry repeats persistence, without repeating Usage application. An applied durable terminal at restore remains finished without another effect or execution holding. Final publication uses its already-staged completion order rather than refreshing an entry aged out during delayed cleanup.
An unconfirmed stop stays in stopUnconfirmed. An ordinary collection that fails without a confirmed terminal provider answer stays in collectionUnconfirmed. These are nonterminal jobs needing attention and keep their resource and receipt through restart. Retrying asks about the existing work, without submitting it again; Dismiss gives the job up without another provider call. Stop journals an accepted receipt and unconfirmed intent before signaling or waiting for an active call and before cancellation, including a renewed receipt. If that intent cannot be saved, no cancel is sent. Direct Stop is tracked through shutdown, and even an expired shutdown grace leaves its intent held across restart. Other attention actions refuse while cancellation is active. A confirmed cancellation or a later collection with a terminal provider answer releases the resource. A cancel answered `finished` or `unknown` — the latter from a provider whose cancel only asks, its next collect telling how the work ended — is followed within the Stop by one collect of that receipt: a success (its outputs let go) or a confirmed failure ends the job cancelled, and a pending reply or an unconfirmed failure stays stopUnconfirmed with the newest receipt, for Retry or Dismiss; there is no loop and no timer. A job on FilmOpen credit the person stopped is confirmed by its operations' End and their receipts (§6.6): every operation settled, the call the Stop cut short or the cancel ends the job cancelled at the receipt's cost, whatever the plug-in answered, with no further cancel or collection; an operation with no receipt confirms nothing. The manager's optional `events` callback is told each step of a Stop as `render.stop` — `requested`, `refused` with its reason (`closed`, `stopping`, `acknowledging`, `unknown`, `over`, `finishing`, `settling`, `stopUnwritten`), `accepted`, `cancelAnswered` with the cancel's `cancelAnswer`, the `decision` (`stopped`, `collect`) and its reason (`confirmedByCancel`, `collectToLearnEnd`), `collectAnswered` with the `cancelAnswer`, the collect's `collectAnswer` (the answer as `render.collected` logs it: its state and the code, stage, `confirmed`, charge and message of a failure, the outputs of a success, or the receipt, phase, progress, wait and charge of a pending reply), the `decision` (`stopped`, `stopUnconfirmed`) and its reason (`finishedOutputsLetGo`, `confirmedEnd`, `unconfirmedFailure`, `stillRunning`), then `completed` or `failed` — with the job, its phase and the milliseconds; a cancel, a collect or the letting go of what it made that throws is `render.cancelFailed` with the job, its `stage` (`cancel`, `collect` or `letGo`), the error's text and type, the decision `stopUnconfirmed`, the outcome `failed` and the milliseconds. A restore is told as `render.restore.start`, `render.restore.read` (whether the journal was read, each restored job's phase, how many entries were dropped unread, the milliseconds), `render.restore.job` per job (its saved and restored phase, the decision — `asSaved`, `pendingEnd`, `preparedSettlement` or the code it was given — its resource and receipt) and `render.restore.done`; an entry dropped unread is told under its code (`render.jobUnread`, `render.pendingEndUnread`, `render.acknowledgementUnread`) with its saved id and phase, the whole entry, the decision `dropped` and the reason (`unknownPhase`, `notAnObject`, `malformed`, `duplicateId`, …). Every other operation is told too, each with its milliseconds: `render.submit.start` (the request) and `render.submit` (the job, its resource and capacity, whether written, or the refusal); `render.admit` for each queued render the queue considers (held, capacity, holding, `admitted` or `waiting` with its reason: `ceiling`, `unclaimed`, `stopped`, `finishing`, `capacity`); `render.send` (`sending` once its intent is written, or why not) and `render.sent`, `render.collect` (`asking` with the receipt, or `skipped` with why) and `render.collected`, each with the answer, its receipt, code, stage and charge; `render.settle` (ask again with its wait and why, attention, or given up); `render.keep` (the facts kept and whether written); `render.finish` (`start`, `preparedWritten`, `settled`, `done`, or `refused` or `failed` with why; each with the settlement payload it carries, a restored one's included, and its milliseconds — the host's replay of that payload logs `usage.settling` with its lines, and `usage.settled` with each record's id, amount and chain as appended or skipped, a skipped one with its saved and logged lines and whether they match, §6.11); `render.acknowledge`; `render.act`; `render.release`; `render.save` (how many entries, each live one's phase, or `failed`); `render.close`. The manager's `report` is given the error itself, and `startRenders` logs a report with the error's type and its message. `startRenders` writes them to the log, a refusal, a failure or an entry dropped unread as a warning. Stop honors a confirmed terminal answer from the active call it waits for without cancelling its old receipt again, and preserves any charge that answer reports. A canonical pending reply resumes ordinary observation with its resource held. The unconfirmed-end marker survives this resumption and restart, so an unanswered later collection returns to the corresponding attention state. A Retry that times out or fails without that answer keeps the attention state, receipt and resource; RenderFailed.confirmed distinguishes a canonical terminal answer from transport failure. A binding refusal before submission has no receipt and ends as failed, releasing normally. RenderAttentionManager exposes generic job views and actions. The application composes them with insertion views using only the insertion-owned validated render id. Tile and Activity preserve insertion identity, placement, progress and presentation while showing that exact render's attention sentence and offered actions. Actions are rechecked and routed to `RenderAttentionManager.act`: Retry collects, replays settlement or acknowledges without submitting; Dismiss gives up that exact hold. An acknowledgement-only insertion subsequently advances its saved marker through its offered ordinary Retry. A true insertion identity refusal still forbids mutation; restoring a server setting alone does not change that identity. When restoration completes, the render manager publishes its loaded snapshot through the existing change stream. Subscribers established before reading see recovery actions without another job event. Closing suppresses a late restoration announcement. After Retry collects, a refused insertion offering Dismiss alone retains its refusal phase and meaning while its exact render still offers Stop; the consumer offers that Stop instead of local dismissal. A Stop-only binding refusal uses the existing action-neutral insertion sentence about the changed film or settings. A stale Dismiss is refused without removing the insertion. ### 6.17 Standalone comparison publishing

`filmopen_comps` is a pure Dart package whose caller supplies a comp's general
JSON, including the picked name, visibility and details, and the ordered media
JSON with each render's supplied metadata and details. Local byte capabilities
are supplied separately by opaque source id; paths and account bearers are not
public fields. Missing costs, providers, stacks and other facts stay null. An
omitted name is the UTC date/time with the supplied generation type. Visibility
defaults to unlisted; unlisted is shared by its link and left out of the public
directory. The draft has no author: the publishing service writes the account's
username, so nobody signs another's name.

The assembler copies originals into a newly owned workspace, measures SHA-256
and byte counts, and generates separate JPEG previews through an injected media
strategy. A native file workspace is a new temporary directory, its access what
the platform gives one; its failures carry where and why as their detail. Portable
preparation decodes bounded still images in an owned native isolate, a PNG's,
JPEG's or WebP's width and height read from its header before any pixel is
decoded, so an image over the pixel ceiling is refused before its pixels are;
an original is the person's own file, so a crafted one's compressed data or
animation frames are not bounded beyond that; a refusal's detail says why,
with the values it decided on, and reaches the log with its code; browser
decoding is synchronous, with queued cancellation delivered before returning
the result. A picture always has a preview; a clip or a sound may have none, and
the portable strategy makes none for a clip, whose facts are the caller's or
unknown, so every kind publishes without a native process. The bundle is the
uploaded `comp.json` first, then the media and their previews; it contains no
HTML or script. Its digest is SHA-256 of the compact inventory of path, bytes,
digest and content type. A prepared bundle names its project (the project's
tag), its slug (15 characters from a secure generator, the unguessable segment of
its address) and its revision; a given revision and slug prepare the same
revision again from the same sources. It permits at most 128 cards, 64 MiB per
original, 512 MiB per bundle, 128 KiB per manifest and 256 KiB per preview. The
caller can only tighten these ceilings. General and per-media details are
bounded JSON objects, copied deeply, with prototype-member keys refused; a
key named like a credential is copied as any other, the service deciding what it
accepts.
Numbers are finite and within the browser's exact integer range; larger
integers use strings. Cancellation releases a stalled source-length future
without waiting for that caller operation to finish. Native mutations cannot
overwrite an existing artifact concurrently. Failed disposal is retryable and
never replaces a preparation diagnostic with a filesystem path.

`CompsPublisher` speaks the publishing contract `/v1`. It receives the existing
account's fresh-token callback, a caller-owned HTTP client and the publishing
host, which the application chooses from its domain alone; it sends over HTTPS
only, follows no redirects, and bounds requests and responses. One deadline
covers the response headers and body, releasing owned response streams on
timeout or cancellation. Upload retries retain the same revision and inventory;
confirmed files are skipped and completion is idempotent. The completed answer
is the published receipt with the comp's public URL; a resume of a published
revision answers that receipt, and its status can be read by revision alone at
any later time. Deleting a published revision is refused by the service with
its receipt, which the publisher answers as published. A refusal the service
names keeps that name as its code, with the HTTP status beside it; any other
keeps a generic code by status. Every failure answered by the service carries
the status and the first 32 KiB of the body for the caller's log.

The assembler and the publisher report every stage to an optional event sink —
each preparation stage with its values and a refusal with its cause, the sizes
and the limits it was decided on; each request before its bearer is attached, each
answer with its status and body, a wait that ended without one with where it
stopped (the token, the headers or the body), the status already received and
why, each retry and refresh with its reason, and each outcome with its elapsed
time — so the caller logs them as they are and no
event holds a credential. No widget, project writer, account initialization or
upload occurs at application startup. `INTEGRATING.md` describes the app-owned
media strategies, the account callback, the events and the prepared bundle's
lifecycle; the package keeps no journal across application restarts, the
caller's record of the revision being the resume.

The application composes it in `lib/comps_composition.dart`, installed off the
web over `filmopen_state`'s `compPosterProvider` (whose default answers every
call *not available* and logs `comp.poster.unavailable` with the call), as the
timeline's engine is installed. A comp record (Project Specification §12.7) maps
to the draft: `name`, `description`, and `visibility` public → listed, private →
unlisted; each item, by its own `n` — never renumbered — is media `m<n>` with
source `s<n>` (a take's stem is no comps id; an item without its number is
refused `bad-item`), its version stem in `details.source`, its kind from the
take's deliverable (a picture deliverable an image, `cl` a video, a sound an
audio) — else from the medium's own file, one of no comps kind refused
`unsupported-format` —, its label the entity's name in use and the deliverable;
its record the one the item names, else the batch its take's name carries,
whichever reference form names the take; from that record's item where it
measured them `width`, `height` and `duration` in seconds — a clip's or a
sound's missing ones then from the selected timeline engine's `probe`, a refused
probe leaving them unknown —; from its render record `model`, `provider` (the
platform), `plugin`, `prompt`, `price` (`cost_usd` in US dollars),
`renderSeconds`, `details.params` and `details.stage` — each absent where the
record lacks it, never zero. Each medium resolves through `ProjectMedia` and the
media store to a local file; a URL, a session copy's bytes, a reference no file
answers to, or a project without a media store is refused `no-file`, and an
unexpected failure `failed`. Posting is two calls, so the caller can write the
record's status `posting` before any request: `prepare` composes and prepares the
bundle in a fresh owned workspace with the portable preparer, sends nothing, and
answers the posting — the comp's stem, revision, slug, the project's tag, the
account signed in, the draft and each source's file — or refuses `no-project`
with no project open and `signed-out` with nobody signed in; `publish` sends it,
the bundle held since `prepare` — released when the publish ends, whatever its
outcome, so no bundle is held across posts and a post is never resumed — or,
where none is held, prepared again for the same revision and slug from the
posting, and refuses `account-mismatch`
without a request unless the posting's account is signed in at its start and is
each token's own: every token the publish asks for is sent only where its
subject (the JWT's `sub`, read, not verified) is the posting's account. `check`
and `abandon` read and delete a revision by its id, asked only as the posting's
account, a revision published first answering its receipt; the comp's page calls
neither, since a post that did not finish is posted again as a new revision
(§9). One post runs at a time, and `cancel` stops it. An outcome carries the receipt's state and URL, or
the refusal's code and the service's status and body, and says whether the
revision's fate is known: a receipt `published` or `cancelled`, or a refusal the
contract calls definite; anything else — a timeout, a server error, an
authentication or quota answer, a local cancel — leaves it unknown. The
publishing host is `publish.comps.<kFilmOpenDomain>`; the token is the account's
`freshAccessToken`, asked for only when a request goes. Each operation logs
`comp.post.start` with its operands before its first wait, `comp.post.step` for
each step and decision — at `compose` each item with the record chosen and why,
each mapped entry with where its kind came from, each probe and what it filled —
and `comp.post.token` as each token is asked for while publishing and as it is
checked, with the posting's account and the token's subject, never the token;
each stage ends with `comp.post` with its stage, outcome, code, detail and
elapsed time and the values it worked on: the stem and the draft whole at
`compose`; the stem, the project, the account, the revision, the slug and the
bytes at `prepare`; at `publish` the stem, revision, slug, the posting's account
and the one signed in, whether the bundle was held, the state, the URL and
whether the fate is known; at `check` and `abandon` the revision, the account
signed in and the outcome. `comp.post.release` names the stem and revision as a publish ends, whether a
bundle was released and the outcome's state or code; `comp.post.cancel` says
whether a post was running, and every event of the package is logged as it is
under `comps.<name>`.
